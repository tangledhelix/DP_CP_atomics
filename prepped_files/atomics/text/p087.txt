The betatron is a relatively new atom gun, and there is little doubt that it
has a bright future in atom-smashing work.

With these atom-smashing machines it became possible to bombard any
material with a variety of particles having energies sufficient to penetrate the
nucleus. As a result many transmutations could be achieved. Thus, modern
alchemy became possible for the first time. These transmutations of common
materials with the atom smashers were to play a vital part in the subsequent
search for large-scale atomic energy.

Better atom guns--present and future . . .

The atom guns that we have described in this chapter are just the forerunners
of atom "cannon" that we are beginning to develop in great variety.
With energies of a few million electron volts, it was found possible to transmute
one atom into another. But with energies of several hundred million
electron volts we approach the range of the cosmic rays and a new and highly
interesting particle called the meson, which seems to play a fundamental role
inside the nucleus. Now that practical atomic energy has been achieved, the
next big job seems to be the study of these mesons and other intimate details of
atomic nuclei. But to do that requires atom cannon producing about 300-M.e.v.
particles. With such machines scientists would not have to depend upon the
accidental high-energy cosmic rays that occasionally create these mesons from
atomic collisions. Therefore, scientists today are busy building new and better
atom smashers to produce these high energies.

A new betatron now being built at the University of Illinois is expected to
produce electrons with several times as much energy as the present 100-M.e.v.
electrons from the biggest betatron.

The cyclotron, too, is in for further development. Recent experiments with
the use of FM (frequency modulated alternating current) have made possible
increased energies. The largest cyclotron in existence has therefore been redesigned.
It is hoped that it will be possible to produce deuterons with energies
of 200-M.e.v., and alphas with 400-M.e.v. energies. Plans for later modifications
have already been made in which it is expected that 350-M.e.v. protons
can be produced.
