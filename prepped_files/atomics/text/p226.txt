With a stationary installation in a central-heating plant, the danger of radiations
harmful to human beings can be eliminated by adequate shielding and
by locating the pile in an isolated spot. Another danger crops up here in that
the water running through the pile will itself become radioactive. The hot
water or steam piped into homes might thus be dangerous to the occupants.
Consequently, it would be necessary for the hot liquid supply leaving the
pile to heat an entirely separate circulation system which would be free of the
radiations. This secondary hot water or steam could then be piped to the
users.

The manner in which the pile could be used to produce motion in
form of engine is also not difficult to foresee. If the temperature of the pile
is permitted to rise high enough to boil water and to produce steam, then we
can obtain our motion by connecting the steam supply thus produced to any
form of steam engine, probably a steam turbine. The most likely use for such
motion would be to operate large generators for the production of electric
