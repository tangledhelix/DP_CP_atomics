could only weed out some of the U238, there would be a higher proportion
of U235 atoms and the chain reaction would be helped considerably. One
attack on the over-all problem then was to enrich the uranium in U235.
As we shall see later, the U235 thus produced could be used in the atomic
bomb.

Impurities in the uranium also tend to be "neutron robbers" and thus
tend to stop the chain. Even traces of the neutron robber, the element boron,
present as an impurity, must be eliminated. It was therefore essential to
have very pure uranium.

Neutrons have to be slowed down . . .

Let us assume that we wish to build a successful atomic-energy pile
using ordinary uranium. We would be disappointed to find out that no
matter how large a piece of pure uranium we take, we cannot keep the
chain going. Why? Because too many neutrons get captured by U238
atoms before they can hit a U235 atom. At first, this appeared to make the
whole problem hopeless. But a few very important discoveries were made.
The neutron that is ejected from a splitting uranium atom is a fast neutron,
moving at speeds of thousands of miles per second. In order to see why a
large lump of ordinary uranium will not sponsor a chain reaction, let us
travel along with a large number of fast neutrons that have just been produced
from splitting uranium atoms. Their energies are somewhat less
than 1 M.e.v. At this stage most of the neutrons make only billiard-ball
collisions with the uranium atoms and their energy is gradually dissipated in
this manner.

But when they have been slowed down to about 30 e.v. they are readily
captured by the uranium 238 atoms. The fortunate neutrons that escape
capture in this energy range continue to bump into uranium atoms until
they are slowed down to only a fraction of an electron volt. Now they are
in much less danger of capture by the uranium 238 atoms and also have an
excellent chance of splitting a U235 atom and helping to keep the chain
going. Of course, all along a few of the U235 atoms are being split by the
neutrons. But so many of the neutrons are captured by the U238 atoms
as they reach the 30-e.v. "trap" that the chain reaction becomes impossible.
This bad situation could be remedied if there were a way in which the fast
neutrons could be slowed down to an energy of only a fraction of an electron
volt without meeting any uranium 238 atoms.
