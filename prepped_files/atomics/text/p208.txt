
What happened at Hiroshima and Nagasaki . . .

One atomic bomb was dropped on the city of Hiroshima, located on the
broad, flat delta of the Ota River, on August 6, 1945. Another was dr
on Nagasaki, one of the largest seaports of southern Japan, on August
1945. Before the bombs dropped, the population of these cities was 255,000
and 195,000. At Hiroshima, 66,000 people died; at Nagasaki 39,000! In all,
there were 135,000 casualties at Hiroshima, while the other doomed city
suffered 64,000 casualties.

General Groves, military head of the atomic-bomb project, described the
damage to these two cities to a Senate committee as follows :

"Practically everything at Hiroshima for a radius of about a mile and a
quarter from the point of detonation was burned as well as blasted. Up to a
radius of two miles from the point of detonation everything was blasted, with
some damage from burning. Between a radius of 2 and 3 miles, everything k
was about half destroyed. Beyond a radius of 3 miles, damage was fairly slight, '
with roof damage up to 5 miles. Glass was broken up to a radius of 12
miles....

"At Nagasaki the blow was struck in a largely industrial area, which left a
large part of the residential area more or less standing. The effects of the explosion
in the industrial area were probably more spectacular and startling
than at Hiroshima; for example the complete destruction of the huge steel
works by blast and fire, and the destruction of the torpedo works by blast alone.
Within a radius of 2,000 feet from the point of detonation, heavy industrial
buildings, gas storage tanks and many reinforced concrete structures were destroyed.
The steel frames in all buildings in all cases were pushed away from
the point of detonation. For a radius of 8,000 feet Japanese workers' homes
were completely demolished. Up to a radius of 2 miles workers' homes had collapsed;
roofs and walls were smashed, but were left partly standing except
in isolated cases where exceptional shielding was given by local topography.
Up to a radius of 3 miles there was some roof damage to tiles of heavy type.
Glass and plaster damage was evident up to much greater distances."

At Hiroshima two-thirds of the buildings were destroyed or severely damaged.
Eighty per cent of the telephone system was destroyed. There were
70,000 breaks in water pipes in homes. Electric wires and trolley cars were
destroyed up to 1 mile from the explosion.

The most frequent cause of death was burns--accounting for about three
cases out of five. About one-third of all deaths were caused by falling debris.
