
Chapter Twenty-one

Energy and Power

The discovery of uranium fission and its peacetime application in the
I pile to produce enormous amounts of energy is a crowning achievement
in mankind's long climb out of the jungle into civilization.
Having become accustomed to various devices developed for our use and
comfort over the past centuries, we have a tendency to take them for granted
and to forget the toil and struggle that went into their creation.

Early steps in energy and power . . .

The first long milestones in this process may seem simple to us, when we
view them with plenty of hindsight. But there is no doubt that each of them
required a long painful process of development. Man did not learn to control
fire in one day. The probabilities are that this control was learned in slow
stages stretching over many centuries.

The wheel seems a very simple thing to us. Yet it undoubtedly took a long
time for men to progress from the simple log used as a roller to the wheel
of the first wagon. With this improvement man could reduce the friction
encountered in pulling something along the ground. In this way he multiplied
by about ten times his ability to move objects. This was no small achievement.
