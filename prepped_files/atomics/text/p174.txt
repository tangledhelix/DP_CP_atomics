Despite the fact that the separation process at Clinton had been designed on
the basis of experiments using only microgram quantities of plutonium, it
worked out successfully. By March, 1944, a fraction of an ounce had been
obtained. Although the plutonium was present in relatively small amounts in
the uranium slug, the separation of this new element from dozens of contaminating
products was achieved with less than a 20 per cent loss by June,
1944. But the ultimate task was far from being completed. Large-scale processes
would have to be developed in order to obtain many pounds of this material.
Professor Smyth reports that, as of the early summer of 1945, the large
plutonium-separating plants at Hanford were operating "with better efficiency
than had been anticipated. The finished product is being delivered."

In summary, then, we might repeat our opening assertion that the separation
of plutonium on a large scale represented a remarkable chemical achievement.
No large-scale plant in history was ever designed on the basis of information
obtained from so tiny a quantity of finished product. A new element had been
produced in quantity. This is more than the mere transmutation of one
known clement into another. It is alchemy leading to elements not recognized
before on this earth. The results of this epoch-making scientific development
will unquestionably alter our modes of living in the future. This alchemy
takes its proper place among the pioneering discoveries that ushered in the
Atomic Age.
