The railroad train must carry along many tons of coal in a separate car, and
frequent stops for refueling are necessary on a long trip. A modern giant plane
on a trip of several thousand miles may use up more weight of fuel than
weight of cargo carried. Our vehicles of today are limited in performance by
the extremely small amount of energy available per pound of fuel, absolutely
insignificant in comparison to the amount of energy we now know they contain.

It's an old story with the sun . . .

The first indication of the enormous amounts of energy available in all
materials came, as with many other scientific discoveries, from astronomy.
Scientists were puzzled by the apparently inexhaustible energy of the sun
and the stars--energy so great that they could not help marveling that no
diminution in heat and light from the sun had ever been noticed. Our puny
earth captures an almost infinitesimal portion of the heat and light radiated
to us from the sun. Yet this is sufficient to keep our globe from freezing; sufficient
to sustain life on earth and to give enormous numbers of plants and
animals the food and energy they need for existence; sufficient to move the
air in winds and the water as rain.

Computation by scientists showed that if the sun were merely a ball of
fire, as originally supposed, obtaining its heat and light from the burning of
fuel, then it could not continue to give off this energy for more than about
6,000 years, a period not much longer than the recorded history of man. Yet
they knew from several other reliable indications that life on earth had
existed for millions of years. We know today that the age of the earth must
be measured in billions of years. Obviously there must be some inexhaustible
reservoir of energy in the sun.

Today we are quite certain that the source of this enormous flood of energy
is the destruction of part of the material of the sun and its conversion into
splashes of energy in almost unbelievable amount. Were we able to destroy
a pound of coal completely and convert it into energy, it would yield 3 billion
times as much energy as when we burn that same pound of coal today.
