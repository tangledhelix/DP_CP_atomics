able to prove that the particle being emitted by the target was a positron. This
was surprising, because all known naturally radioactive substances emitted
alpha particles or high-speed electrons (beta particles).

The arithmetic of this historic discovery . . .

Ingenious studies by the Joliots led them to state the atomic collision in this
way:

Alpha particle + aluminum --> phosphorus + neutron
(bullet)         (target)

neutrons    2   13      15    0
protons     2   14      15    1
           --   --      --   --
            4   27      30    1
           -------      -------
weight        31     =     31

The phosphorus produced in this collision with an atomic weight of 30 is not
stable. It breaks down in the following manner with the ejection of a positron:

Phosphorus --> silicon + positron
(parent)  (daughter)

protons      15     14
neutrons     15     16
             __      __
weight       30  =  30

Although the details of this breakdown of unstable phosphorus are not
yet well understood, it is helpful to consider that the proton might be able to
split into a neutron and a positron. The positron is ejected with high velocity.
As a result the nucleus now has a positive charge one smaller than it had
originally. A new atom of atomic number 14, silicon, is produced. This silicon
atom of atomic weight 30 is a stable atom found in nature. And so the nuclear
breakdown comes to a halt.

What proof could the Joliots offer in favor of this nuclear rearrangement?
Several lines of evidence could be used. The aluminum-foil target was made
part of the cloud chamber. During the alpha-particle bombardment, tracks of
atoms hit by the neutrons could be identified. Positron tracks were also
observed. But when the alpha-particle source was taken away, the traces due
to the neutron collisions disappeared. The number of positron traces, however,
did not disappear immediately but gradually diminished in the manner
mentioned above.
