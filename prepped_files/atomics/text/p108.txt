If you are in a room in which there is a near-by radium-beryllium source of
neutrons, you will discover that the silver coins in your pocket have become
radioactive. The event that has taken place in the core of the silver atom may
be written in this manner:

Neutron (bullet) + silver (target) --> radioactive silver

Then the silver nucleus ejects a beta particle :

Radioactive silver --> cadmium + beta particle

In this way silver is being transmuted into cadmium. Some of the gold in a
coin of this more precious metal becomes converted to mercury (the next
higher element) under these conditions. Dozens of other examples of this
type might be given.

There is thus no doubt that transmutations of the elements arc readily effected
by neutrons. The alchemists would have been successful (at least on a
microscopic scale) had they had this tool of the modern physicist. The actual
amount of transmutation is very small under ordinary laboratory conditions,
even when a neutron beam from a fair-sized cyclotron source is used. Even in
an entire day, the amounts would probably not be weighable.

There are other types of encounters that can occur with neutrons, but the
type mentioned above is probably the most important. A neutron hitting a
boron atom might result in the formation of lithium and helium (alpha particle).
On striking a nitrogen atom, a carbon atom and a hydrogen atom might
be formed.

Gold is not the object of modern alchemy . . .

development of this century, the operation of an atomic-energy machine,
commonly called a pile. We say little here concerning this because the latter
part of this book is largely devoted to its description. At this point we shall
simply state that the operation of such a pile makes it possible to obtain a
beam of neutrons so intense that "practical alchemy" is actually being carried
on. What is the prize? Not gold, or platinum, but a great variety of radioactive
