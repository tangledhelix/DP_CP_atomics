
Helium + nitrogen --> oxygen + hydrogen

neutrons    2    7       8    1
protons     2    7       9    0
           --   --      --   --
            4   14      17    1
           -------      -------
weight        18     =     18
charge      2 + 7    =    8 + 1
            -----         -----
              9             9

What do we learn from this arithmetic--which most of us will admit is
much simpler than filling out an income-tax blank? We see clearly that there
has been a rearrangement among the protons and neutrons that make up the
atomic nuclei. We can also see that the total charge is the same before and
after and that the total number of protons and neutrons has not changed.
A transmutation has taken place, because different elements are present before
and after the collision. It appears as if the high-speed alpha particle was
actually able to enter the nucleus of the nitrogen atom. This probably resulted
in an intolerable congestion and instability. Consequently a proton was ejected
and an oxygen atom of weight 17 was left. This is a stable, although uncommon,
chemical twin of oxygen.

Energy was omitted in the boofyecping . . .

In the above discussion we omitted a significant product of the atomic collision,
namely, the large amount of energy. This appeared in the form of the
high speed with which the proton left the scene of the collision--as did the
fast-moving oxygen atom. Thus the resulting collision not only produces a new
atom but releases a large amount of energy as well. In this action we thus
obtain a glimpse into the potentialities of transmuting one element into another
and simultaneously releasing large amounts of energy per atom.

But lest our enthusiasm run away with us at this point, we must bear in
mind that we are here dealing with one atom. In order to get one alpha particle
to hit the "fly in the cathedral" it is necessary to shoot about 1 million alpha
particles into the nitrogen. The amount of energy we get out of the process is
far smaller than the amount of energy we put in. But the very fact that human
beings could actually smash even one atom and transmute it into another
one with a release of a large amount of energy per atom provided a tremendous
stimulus to hosts of scientists all over the world. They began to bombard
every conceivable substance with every conceivable particle, with any source
of high-speed particles they could obtain. The result of these efforts has been
to discover a large number of artificial transmutations of one atom into another.
