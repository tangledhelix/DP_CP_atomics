Perhaps its terrible destructiveness can be brought home by picturing what
would happen if an atomic bomb hit any large city familiar to you. Let us
assume that this bomb were dropped from an airplane so as to explode about 1/4
mile in the air--the approximate height of the bomb bursts at Hiroshima,
Nagasaki, and the first Bikini bomb. Almost immediately, a wave of heat
would sweep out from the center of the bomb, traveling with the speed
light. Following the first intense flash lasting a fraction of a second, there
would be a prolonged period of about 10 to 15 seconds during which an
enormous amount of heat and light would be given off. At a distance of 6
miles, this would seem equivalent in light and heat-radiating power to several
times the brilliance of our sun. At a distance of 1 mile it would be as hot
and as bright as 50 to 100 suns!

Within the first fraction of a second, a person standing 2 miles away in the
direct rays of this "sun" would have the surface temperature of his skin
heated to about 150° F. At closer distances, the heating effect would be even
greater. The result would be a most severe instantaneous "sunburn" which
would be fatal in many cases. The heat would be so intense that wooden poles
would char up to 2 miles away. Dry combustible materials for miles around
would immediately catch fire, creating a raging fire equivalent to a mass
incendiary-bomb raid by a large number of planes. Fires would be caused in
this manner up to 3 miles away. The heat from the flash would be so intense
that polished granite would be roughened expansion of the surface.
Roof tiles would be melted and bubbled at a distance of 1 mile from the
bomb.

As a result of the fires caused by the initial heat of the bomb, "fire-storm"
winds of 30 to 40 miles per hour would blow for hours afterward in a giant
"draft" caused by the burning houses.

A few seconds after the initial flash, a terrific blast wave would strike. At a
distance of 1-1/4 mile, winds of tornado force would rage. About 2-1/2 miles
from the bomb, winds the size of a gale--sufficient to knock over billboards
and light roofs--would be felt. A 9-inch wall about 3/4 mile away would be
blown over by the blast.
