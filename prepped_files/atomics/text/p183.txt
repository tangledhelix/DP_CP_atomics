
URANIUM

SEPARATOR

Chapter Seventeen

U235

With the transmutation of uranium 238 into plutonium, man's
list of fissionable materials was increased to at least four elc-jnents--plutonium,
uranium, thorium, and protactinium. Of
these, only two seemed suitable as bomb material, plutonium and U235. In
order to ensure success in the event that one of these proved unsuitable either
in the production of the material or in its fabrication into a bomb, large-scale
manufacture of U235 was undertaken simultaneously with the production of
plutonium.

At first glance, the task of large-scale separation of U235 from U238 would
seem impossible. The difference in atomic weight is so small as to be practically
insignificant, amounting to little more than 1 per cent. And there
is only 1 atom of U235 in 140 atoms of natural uranium. Success in this
attempt at separation would seem to be nearly impossible with the limited
time available in a war period. Yet the job was accomplished.

To ensure success in this difficult task of separating U235 from U238, at
least four methods of production were tried out. Of these the gas diffusion
method turned out to be one of the most successful.
