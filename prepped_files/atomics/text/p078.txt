
Chapter Eight

Atom Guns

The citadel of atomic structure has high ramparts. When positively
charged particles are approaching the nucleus, intensive repulsive
forces come into play. In order to penetrate these "walls of repulsive
forces" high-speed projectiles must be used. The atomic scientist has designed
for this purpose a number of different types of "atom guns" which shoot high-speed
charged projectiles capable of penetrating the protective walls around
the nucleus.

According to our model, an atom consists of a very small core, or nucleus,
which contains almost all the weight. In addition, there is an outer or planetary
shell containing electrons. The volume of this shell is many thousand times
greater than that of the nucleus.

Now consider the plight of a proton or alpha particle used in an attempt to
penetrate into this core. As it approaches the outer diffuse layers of the atom
which contain the electrons, it experiences little interference with its motion.
The projectile is much heavier than the electrons and can penetrate the outer
electron layers as easily as a bowling ball could penetrate a "guard" of marbles
placed around the bowling pins.
