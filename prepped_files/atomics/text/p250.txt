The dangerous radioactivity produced
near an operating uranium-graphite pile and the burns produced by
radium might lead one to wonder about the possibility of the harmful effects
of these radioactive atoms in an animal or plant. Is it safe to feed these
spy doses to human "guinea pigs"?

It happens that these experiments can be made in a harmless manner. Bear
in mind that the Geiger counter and similar instruments can detect individual
radioactive atoms, while the smallest piece of matter that we can weigh contains
many million millions of ordinary stable atoms. Thus, valuable studies
can be made in which the sample of tracer material contains only one radioactive
atom per billion. A billionth of an ounce of radioactive iodine can be
used to make an effective tracer study in a human being.

Many experiments have indicated the dosage of radioactive substances that
can be taken with safety by human beings. This dosage can usually be kept
at tiny fractions of dangerous values, with definite detection still possible
by our sensitive instruments. Thus, a variety of experiments with an assortment
of radioactive atoms is possible with plants, animals, and human beings
without disturbing normal functioning.
