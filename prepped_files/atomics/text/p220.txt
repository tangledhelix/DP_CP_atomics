
A challenge: harnessing the sun's bounty . . .

This is indeed a treasure house of energy. When shall we learn how to
harness efficiently this horsepower per square yard? Some have tried it with
giant concave mirrors that focus the light and heat of the sun upon a container
of water. The steam produced runs a steam engine which operates a generator.
In turn, the generator charges up a storage battery. This can then be used for
lighting lamps and running motors. But the efficiency is so low that enormous
mirrors are needed, constantly rotating so as to face the sun and larger than
the roofs of most houses. Nevertheless, the search for more efficient utilization
of this direct storehouse of the sun's energy goes on. In the meantime, nature
has provided us with a number of indirect ways of using tiny amounts of the
sun's energy in order to run our machinery and heat our homes.

Part of the energy which the earth captures heats the water in the oceans
and lifts evaporated vapor high in the air. This moisture, condensing as rain,
falls down the mountain and hillside streams where we can capture some of
the energy of motion with a water wheel. Or we can dam up the source
of water and obtain the energy of the falling water to operate giant turbines,
turn dynamos, and generate electric energy.

Another part of this heat energy from the sun warms the air and causes
giant convection currents on earth familiar
to us as the winds. This moving
air can then be used by us to operate
windmills, push sailboats across the
seas, or even to turn generators on our
farms for the operation of lights and
small motors.
