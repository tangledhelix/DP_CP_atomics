There is also a blood disease, called polycythemia, in which too many red
blood corpuscles are produced. As a result the blood becomes thick and the
circulation rate is reduced. Radioactive phosphorus has shown itself to be the
best available remedy for this disease.

Success in treatment of specific cancers and other diseases with radioactive
iodine and phosphorus is stimulating other attempts along the same line. Thus,
experiments are now proceeding to find ways of attaching radioactive at
to sex hormones which lodge in specific places such as the breast and uterus in
women, and the prostate gland in men. It is hoped that such radioactive
hormones can then be administered to persons having cancers of these organs
with the possibility that the presence of the radioactivity within the organ
may suppress the growth of cancer cells.

Atomic science and some problems of life . . .

Biologists have recently begun to probe fundamental questions of life, and
some indications point to a connection between these important problems
and atomic science.

During the research on radiation effects near atomic piles, it was discovered
that many animals which were placed near large amounts of radiation died
of "old age," although youthful in terms of years. The question naturally
arises as to the effect on our life span of the cosmic radiation that passes
through us every day of our lives. Might this be a factor in the premature
aging of man? This is purely a speculative question but one that may turn
out to have important bearing in the future.

Atomic science may also play an important part in the field of genetics,
where questions of the development of new species are considered. Several
decades ago it was discovered that x-rays have the ability to create mutations
animals and plants with new characteristics. These mutations breed true to
these new characteristics and thus give rise to new breeds. With plentiful
supplies of fast and slow neutrons, gamma radiations, and x-rays of any desired
frequency, experimentation in this field may yet yield rich rewards.
