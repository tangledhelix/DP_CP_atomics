The extremely large value of the velocity of light results in the production
of a very large amount of energy for an exceedingly small loss in mass. Let us
express this relation in terms of units with which we are more familiar.
For mass, we shall use the pound. For energy, let us use the kilowatt-hour.
We are more familiar with this unit because our consumption of electric
energy in the home is rated in units of kilowatt-hours. If you read a book
with the aid of a 100-watt bulb for 10 hours, the electrical energy consumed
is 1 kilowatt-hour. In terms of these units, the Einstein relation may be
written

Energy in kilowatt-hours = 11400,000,000 times the mass in pounds

In other words, the annihilation of a 1-pound mass results in the liberation of
11.4 billion kilowatt-hours of energy!

Quantities of energy almost beyond the imagination . . .

Energy quantities of this magnitude are indeed difficult for us to comprehend.
All we can do is provide a few examples showing the use of this
equation.

a kilowatt-hour of electric energy

Compare this with the fuel value
of 1 pound of coal when burned,
approximately 8 kilowatt-hours instead
of 114 billion (11400,000,000).
The value of this energy from
a pound of coal, at the rate of 3
