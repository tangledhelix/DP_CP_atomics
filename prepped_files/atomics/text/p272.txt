incorporating winged rockets predicted an increase of range to 300 miles for
V-2 by finishing the trajectory in a glide and an increase to 3,000 miles for
rockets of the 100-ton class. This phenomenal increase in range was based on a
trajectory in which the rocket would bounce out of the lower atmosphere
into the stratosphere in a succession of jumps (like a stone skipping on water)
and end in a glide. Extensions of these techniques to rockets of more than
two stages permit increasing the range indefinitely with the progressive decrease
in percent of payload.

"Very long range rockets were not a serious threat before the atomic bomb
because of the high ratio of weight--and consequently the cost--of the vehicle
compared to the explosive load. Even a two stage rocket would barely break
even economically--the cost of destroying a square mile by this means being
comparable with, if not larger than, the damage done to the enemy. With an
atomic warhead, however, the cost of the carrier is not excessive and even the
expense of the most elaborate guiding and controlling equipment would not
make the total product inefficient, for the destructive effect would exceed the
total cost by a large factor.

"The problem of defense against the V-2 was unsolved during the war, and
although interception by ground-launched rockets was theoretically possible,
the practical difficulties would take years to overcome. The pick-a-back rocket
and other offensive improvements could, on the other hand, be developed in
much less time."

Shall we bury our cities underground? . . .

Once rockets or planes have penetrated any defenses that might be set up
a nation, the only protection to its cities would be the wide dispersal of
factories and homes, together with a system of extremely deep shelters and
underground facilities. All these measures can only be partially effective,
short of a complete alteration in our mode of living. The effect of these steps
on our lives would be most profound, assuming that we wished to live such a
life as is implied in this burrowing underground. Even with all such measures
we can only partially stave off the increased destructiveness of atomic weapons,
radioactive "poison" sprays, and new forms of germ warfare.
