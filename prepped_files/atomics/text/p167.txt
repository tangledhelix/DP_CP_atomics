In order to produce the plutonium needed for atomic-energy release, it
was necessary to construct uranium piles enormously larger than the small
pile using 6 tons of uranium that we described in our previous chapter. In
all these larger piles, the heat developed is great enough to melt the entire
pile if care is not taken to cool it properly. This problem of removing the
heat energy produced became one of the main stumbling blocks in the
manufacture of plutonium. We shall see later how this problem was met.

Three uranium-carbon chain-reacting piles were built during the Second
World War in the United States in addition to the first experimental pile
at Chicago already described. These piles are historic achievements and will
rank in the history of civilization along with the first steam engine, the first
motor, and the first electric power plant.

The Argonne pile . . .

This chain-reacting atomic-energy machine was constructed in 1943 in the
Argonne Laboratory outside Chicago, under the able direction of Enrico
Fermi, assisted by a corps of scientists. The pile was very similar to the
original uranium-graphite pile built in the football stadium of the University
of Chicago already described above. The cubical structure was surrounded
by a suitable shield to protect the personnel from the radiations that were
produced. Since it had no efficient cooling system, it was operated at a reasonably
low power level. This pile normally liberated only a few kilowatts of
heat energy and consequently could not be used for the production of sizable
quantities of plutonium. However, it served many useful purposes. Operation
of the pile furnished Fermi and his group with much useful information that
was to be applied later to the construction of larger piles. In addition, a uniform
beam of neutrons could be obtained from an opening at the top of the pile.
Using this beam, many important scientific results were obtained.

Another pile was later built at the Argonne Laboratory using heavy water
instead of carbon as a moderator. This achievement will be described in
the following chapter, which deals with heavy water.
