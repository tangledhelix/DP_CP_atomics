The new medical research . . .

Our school catalogues attempt to compartmentalize nature by offering
courses in physics, chemistry, biology, mathematics, medicine, etc. This is an
artificial subdivision, dictated purely by convenience. The functioning of
living organisms is a complex interplay of physical and chemical processes in
a biological system. The cooperative effort of physicists, chemists, biologists,
physicians, and mathematicians, exploited as a team, will have to be marshaled
to make significant advances. Look at the staff of a large chemical
plant--chemical engineers, physical chemists, organic chemists, physicists,
analysts, and metallurgists. A large drug house will also enlist the teamed
services of physicians, bacteriologists, and entomologists. As science and
industry progress, the role of the "lone wolf" working in a small cellar laboratory
will gradually diminish. The cooperative functioning of the body will
have to be attacked by cooperative effort among many specialized groups.

If the combined efforts of thousands of chemists, physicists, biologists, engineers,
and mathematicians and the expenditure of 2 billion dollars of our
national income could achieve the miracle of atomic energy, there is no
doubt that a similar expenditure of brains and money can perform equal
miracles in the prolongation of life. Even a fraction of the cost of the atomic
bomb will produce results that will save thousands and perhaps millions of
people untold future suffering from disease. If the coordinated scientific attack
as exemplified in the atomic-bomb project can be applied to medicine, there
is little doubt that many of the ailments that afflict mankind can be cured
or prevented. Such an attack will make substantial inroads toward our goal
of eliminating the common cold, infantile paralysis, tuberculosis, heart disease,
cancer, and the other plagues besetting us.

Medicine thus stands on the threshold of a new era in its development, as
revolutionary as that caused in atomic science by the discovery of radium and
the x-ray. It is only a matter of time before the fruits of this new medical
era begin to ripen into manifold blessings for mankind.
