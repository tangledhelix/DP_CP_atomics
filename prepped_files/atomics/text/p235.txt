Even if atomic-energy power plants were not to replace existing coal-burning
plants in toto, there seems to be some field for use as auxiliary power plants.
Smaller, standardized atomic-energy piles might be placed at strategic points
in a utility-company system to serve as a constant supply of power, with coal-burning
plants used to take up the peak periods of power production at proper
times during the day. This would ensure partial operation of the system at
many points to guard against some major failure at the main power house.

What about power plants for battleships, huge commercial ships, and aircraft?
Atomic power plants for the large ocean-going vessels are a development
very near at hand. Government contracts for investigating this problem
have already been announced. It has been estimated that it will take about 5
to 10 years before such power plants can be actually installed. In the case of
large aircraft the problems appear to be most difficult to solve. On the other
hand, it is this application which could dispense with the burden of fuel
transportation--so large a percentage of total cargo in this case. Contracts to
several aviation companies have been announced in which this possibility
will be explored.

There seems little doubt, given peace and friendly cooperative effort on the
part of all nations, that our generation will be fortunate enough to be among
the pioneers in the application of this new source of power.
