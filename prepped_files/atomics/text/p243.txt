Atomic energy from more plentiful atoms . . .

Up to this point man has uncovered four methods by which atomic energy
is released in nature. On the earth, we have the naturally radioactive materials,
which exist in quantities too small to be utilized for any but highly specialized
purposes. We have the chain-reacting uranium pile as the only practical large-scale
liberation of atomic energy to date. It is also possible to manufacture artificially
radioactive materials with the pile or with atom guns. A fourth process,
the release of atomic energy in the sun and stars, arouses our envy and admiration.
This process not only utilizes common materials such as hydrogen to
liberate energy but is about ten times as efficient as well. While the use of U235
in a chain reaction converts only 1 part in a thousand of the mass into energy,
in the stars about 1 part in a hundred of the mass of hydrogen is converted
to energy. Were we able to duplicate this process on earth, then there would be
absolutely limitless energy available. The vast quantities of water on earth,
close at hand, would provide infinitely more hydrogen than we could profitably
use. Could we ever duplicate this energy conversion on the earth?

Let's see how the sun does it. According to a theory by H. Bethe, the hydrogen
is converted into helium by a complex series of steps involving carbon and
hydrogen isotopes as follows:

1. Carbon (12) captures hydrogen (1) --> nitrogen (13)

2. Nitrogen (13) breaks down --> carbon (13) and positron

3. Carbon (13) captures hydrogen (1) --> nitrogen (14)

4. Nitrogen (14) captures hydrogen (1) --> oxygen (15)

5. Oxygen (15) breaks down --> nitrogen (15) and positron

6.  Nitrogen (15) captures hydrogen (1) --> carbon (12) and helium (4)
