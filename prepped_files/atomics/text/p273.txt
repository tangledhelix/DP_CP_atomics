
With the increased value of surprise attack, it is probable that atomic bombs
and planes would be kept by each nation in the most secluded places to avoid
detection. Thus, despite an attempt at destruction of one nation's cities by
another, retaliation could be effected by returning the attack. Therefore, if a
spirit of mutual mistrust is not checked, atomic bombs will be made and
stored by many nations until each has accumulated stock piles large enough
to paralyze their potential enemies if used. It is difficult to predict what might
happen under these conditions. A nation, suspicious of the motives of another
nation, may launch an all-out atomic-bomb attack with great suddenness. The
mere existence of atomic bombs possessed by any one nation in the world
would therefore stimulate atomic-bomb production in every nation on earth
capable of the industrial effort necessary. If unchecked, such an atomic-bomb
race would rapidly bring suspicions to fever pitch and generate a situation
where the peoples of the earth would live in mortal fear of one another. Such
an atmosphere would very likely provoke some one nation to push the buttons
launching atomic war.

Only the insects, earthworms, and buzzards stand to benefit from such a
calamity.

Fundamental principles of the atomic bomb are not secret . . .

In 1940 all that was known about the possibility of an atomic bomb centered
about the knowledge that uranium could undergo fission, that it could
produce large amounts of energy per atom, and that fission might produce
enough neutrons to continue a chain reaction.

It took little more than a year to establish in the laboratories that the growth
factor could be made greater than 1. By the end of 1942 the first uranium pile
had actually been operated from principles established in the laboratory. Every
scientist on earth now knows that such an atomic-energy machine is possible.
He also knows the general method of operation of such a pile. It is doubtful
whether it would take more than a year for any of the major nuclear research
laboratories in many nations to duplicate this machine. The status of nuclear
research in other nations is not so far behind ours that they could not repeat
what we have done, especially since we have shown that it is possible. All in
all, it seems quite likely that several nations already have operating piles.

With these piles scientists can determine the basic theory of construction
of the atomic bomb. Larger piles can then be built for manufacture of plutonium,
or plants for separation of U235 can be constructed. Scientists of
other lands will have time to build pilot plants and semi-works with which
to refine the final construction details and avoid some of our costly processes.
