Other features of a uranium-carbon pile . . .

What additional features might we notice as we make a mind's eye tour
of a proj ected power plant? It has been located in a strategic region, so as not
to duplicate power that could readily be obtained from coal or water power
in the vicinity. The original loading of the pile may have been natural uranium
and thorium. As the power plant operates, however, it may be necessary to
add some U235 or plutonium from time to time by remote control. This
would be the case if, for example, the pile produced less than 1 pound of
plutonium while "burning" 1 pound of U235. Of course, a much happier
situation would arise if more than 1 pound of plutonium or fissionable material
from thorium could be produced by burning a pound of U235. Then the
large central power plant could produce fissionable materials as a by-product
and sell these to smaller power plants or to users who preferred to use plutonium
in smaller piles.

This power plant differs markedly from the coal-steam-electricity generating
plants with which we are familiar. Considerable chemical activity in side
plants must be carried on. There are several operations that can be accomplished
here. The uranium slugs are gradually becoming contaminated with
the products of fission. Some of these are powerful neutron robbers and tend
to stop the chain reaction. Consequently these must be periodically removed
by chemical processing from the "used" uranium. At the same time, the
plutonium could be separated if desired. The purified uranium must eventually
be returned to the pile in order to have an efficient process.

materials. Yet we find that one branch of this plant is a storehouse of alchemic
wonders. The intensity of the neutron beams obtainable from the

the largest cyclotron source. Samples of target material are placed in the
