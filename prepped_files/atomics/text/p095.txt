First man-made transmutation opened up wide vistas . . .

These and a host of other transmutations opened up the possibility of producing
rare materials more cheaply by transmutation from one element to
another than by searching out some raw material on the earth from which it
could be made. In fact, the manufacture of plutonium (element 94) in the
making of the atomic bomb was a practical application of the use of this process
of transmutation of elements on a large and practical scale. Here was the
artificial production of an element which in its natural state may have disappeared
long ago as a result of radioactive disintegration.

At the time of Rutherford's pioneering experiment in 1919, these were
extremely remote possibilities. Today, less than 30 years later, the large-scale
production of elements by man-made transmutation is a reality. In the remaining
chapters we shall describe in greater detail the results of one of mankind's
greatest ventures--modern alchemy.

By acquiring the ability to study transmutations in a controlled manner,
we have also been led to a verification of the extremely important relation
between matter and energy. This relation will help us understand the liberation
of large-scale atomic energy on the earth and in the stars.

Matter can be transformed into energy . . .

In the simple exercise in arithmetic which we carried out in the case of the
transmutation above, it seemed as if the mass was the same before and after
the collision of the alpha particle with the nitrogen atom. But when the exact
atomic weights are inserted, it is found that the "books do not exactly balance."
Take the case where the products formed by the collision leave the scene with
a great deal of energy. Then it is found that the mass of the products is slightly
less than that of the original materials. Where has the missing mass gone?
The answer is simple to state but startling in its implications. The matter has
been annihilated, and in its place has been created an equivalent amount of
energy. It is by such an annihilation of matter that the energy of the exploding
atomic bomb and the radiation from the stars are produced.
