Maying the uranium-carbon pile more efficient . . .

At first glance, we might conclude that the uranium-carbon pile is rather
inefficient because only 1 atom in 140 is U235. It is the splitting of this atom
which yields the energy desired. But actually the U238 atoms are not simply
carried along as dead weight.

Looking ahead to the time when atomic power will be a sharply competitive
industry, there seems little doubt that the ingenuity of our inventors
will be directed toward the utilization of the entire sample of natural uranium.

It is interesting in this connection to review the story of our great petroleum
industry and its efforts to convert as much as possible of the crude petroleum
into gasoline "fractions." At an early date, kerosene was the desired material
and the gasoline fractions were actually thrown out. With the advent of the
automobile engine, the small gasoline fraction present in crude petroleum
began to be used. Soon it became possible by the familiar cracking process to
convert the larger molecules present in the kerosene into gasoline. Then, by
the process of polymerization, efforts were made to convert the lighter molecules
into gasoline. As a result, the amount of gasoline now obtainable from
crude petroleum has increased by a severalfold factor. Of course, this example
