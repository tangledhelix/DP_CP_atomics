destroy our military targets, in what way could we keep ahead of this enemy?
Atomic bombs are different. Enough can be made to destroy completely all
possible targets and kill the inhabitants of all major cities of any country. It
is then impossible to destroy them twice or to kill people twice. Eventually
therefore, we cannot hope to keep ahead of other countries in an atomic war.

It is everyone's problem . . .

The emergence of atomic energy has catapulted into prominence today a
problem that might otherwise have tarried along for 10, 20, or 50 years. This
problem is posed for us today with the keenness of a razor's edge. The alternatives
seem so terrible in one direction--so bountiful in the other. It is for us
to decide if we are to use atomic energy for good or for evil, for construction
or for destruction, for the elevation of mankind or for his degradation. We
may delay consideration of other problems, but we cannot tarry in this case
where the alternatives are so pressing.

Some editorial writers have pointed out that modern man has become
obsolete now that his technical and destructive powers have outraced his
social and moral progress. It is necessary for all of us to begin examining
thoroughly the causes of war and destruction, and to bend every effort to
eliminate them before they destroy us.

Arthur H. Compton, Nobel prize winner in physics and one of the leaders
of the atomic-bomb project, has summed up a great deal of discussion in
pointing out that "recent scientific developments have proved conclusively
that love of one's neighbor has become the rule of life. We cannot survive
without it."
