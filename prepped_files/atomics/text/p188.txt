
What industrial corporation (wishing to remain in business) would go
ahead under peacetime conditions with the construction of the plant when
no solution to the problem of barrier design was yet known? Probably none!
Yet construction under government funds went ahead. Research on good barriers
(or diffuscrs) went ahead simultaneously and a successful solution was
worked out in time.

Numerous additional problems that arose were solved. According to a report
from the Kellex Corporation which operated the plant, the process used
more pumps, more diffusion barriers, more power, and more instruments on
which were concentrated more research and more money, than have ever been
expended on a single project.

Totalling about 70 buildings and sprawling over 600 acres at Oak Ridge,
Tennessee, a $500,000,000 plant was built, creating almost overnight an en
tirely new industry based on gaseous diffusion through porous barriers. Gaseous
diffusion was practically a laboratory tool, never having been employed
on a commercial scale--or even on a scale approaching a single stage of the
envisioned plant at Oak Ridge. . . . New coolants, piping, heat exchangers,
lubricants, pumps, packing, seals, valves, instruments, etc., were developed
to satisfy the stringent requirements. . . . Several thousand new and improved
products and procedures are now available to American industry, awaiting
only government release. . . . For the first time pump operation at velocities
greater than the speeed of sound has been attained--a milestone in pumping
history." (Emphasis ours.)

If all the 70 buildings were combined into one, it would create a structure
1/2 mile long, 1/4 mile wide, and four stories high. The largest steam power
plant in history was used for this one plant; it supplied almost a quarter million
kilowatts of power and steam to keep the diffusion process in operation.

The amazing thing about this accomplishment is the fact that it was so
successful despite the elimination of many of the usual pilot-plant and semi-works
stages required in normal industrial advances. It had to be planned so
carefully at the drawing-board stage that the final plant could operate successfully
despite the elimination of customary procedure.

At the same time, the lessons learned in building the gas-diffusion plant
will have great application to the know-how of modern industry and will bear
fruit in many as yet unforeseen ways.
