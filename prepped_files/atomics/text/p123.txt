What kind of particle was it? Was it positively or negatively charged?
Now that the direction of motion was established, this question could be
answered at once. From the known direction of the magnetic field that was
being applied, the particle was deflected in the direction that a positive particle
would be. In this particular arrangement a negative particle would be deflected
in the opposite (that is, clockwise direction, looking at the diagram). Hence
it was a positive particle.

What was it--a proton, an even heavier positive particle such as an alpha
particle, or something entirely new? If it were a proton, then the energy of
the proton leaving the lead plate could be calculated from the curved nature
of the track. But such a calculation based on known proton tracks showed that
a proton of this energy could travel only one-tenth the distance shown in the
diagram after passing through the lead plate. In other words, it could not be

such as an alpha particle. Furthermore, the thinness of the track resembled
