
Chapter Twenty-two

Harnessing the Atom

A fascinating challenge . . .

Although the United States has for many years been the largest harnesser
of power in the world, the impact of the atom-bomb disclosure has
had staggering effects on our imaginative dreams for the future.
Newspaper headlines and magazine articles have told us what minute quantities
of uranium 235 would be consumed in performing power tasks in the
home and factory. There seems little doubt that these headlines are not fantastic
if we peer far enough into the future. But, in the same manner that a
play-by-play account of an exciting game is far more interesting than simply
knowing the final score, we shall find the most dramatic news latent in the
answers to the questions, How will atomic energy be harnessed for power
purposes? To what extent will it replace water and steam power? With what
barriers is the controlled use of atomic energy apparently guarded?

As we shall soon discover, it is not as if we had simply unlocked a limitless
mine--a mine of energy--and all we need do is to reach down and take it out.
Many thorns lie in our path. The longest and sharpest of these are the highly
dangerous radiations that accompany the liberation of this energy. Nevertheless,
we do not believe that these problems are insoluble.
