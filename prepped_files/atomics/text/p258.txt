The earliest types of cancer attacked in this manner were those on or very
close to the skin. At first, the radium was enclosed in a metallic shield and
the radiations directed through a tiny hole in this shield. But radium was
very scarce and this procedure tied up the available supply so much that only
a limited number of cases could be treated. A great step forward was made
when it was discovered that radon gas, formed from the breakdown of a
radium atom, was also effective in treatment of cancer. This gas was collected
in thin gold needles or capsules and sunk deep into the cancerous tissue. The
half-life of radon is less than 4 days, and as a result the penetrating gamma
radiations quickly decrease and die out. The use of this radon gas instead of
the radium itself reduced the expense and difficulty of the cancer treatment
and freed valuable radium for other purposes.

By this means, many wonderful cures were effected. Early malignancies of
the breast could be checked. In the relatively frequent cancers of the womb,
the long needles could be left for anywhere from a day to a week. It was found
that needles shot into the tissues surrounding the growths had remarkable
checking power. This technique was used for cancers of the tongue, nose, and
lips. Aided by skillful surgical methods, incisions could be made that would
expose the stomach and allow the insertion of the healing needles. Growths
in the liver and intestines were likewise checked by these means.

What is occurring to produce this beneficial effect? In general, the action
of all penetrating radiation is to disrupt and shatter the molecules of the
chemical ingredients making up the living tissue. In the case of superficial
and highly localized growths, the patient can tolerate the destruction of the
entire tissue mass. But a more desirable situation would occur if the radiation
could show preferential destruction of the diseased tissue and thus tend to
preserve the healthy cells.

At a time when radium was the only material available, there was little
that could be done in a case where a cancer resisted such preferential destruction.
Now, however, with the possibility of creating a large number of artificially
radioactive materials in the uranium-graphite pile, a wide range of
treatments is available. Each of these radioactive materials will differ in its
radiations, thus opening up the possibility of finding a preferentially destructive
radiation for many types of cancers that now resist treatment. For example,
in a recent experiment radioactive phosphorus was found effective in the
treatment of skin cancers.
