As if nature had planned to lend a helping hand to our quest for controlled
atomic energy, it was observed that the ejection of some neutrons
from split uranium atoms is slightly delayed. As a result, we are granted
considerably more time with which to manipulate the controls of an atomic-energy
machine in case it should be getting out of hand.

Keeping the chain letters from going to waste . . .

If the mass of uranium is too small or if the size of the atomic-energy
machine is too small, then many neutrons ejected from a splitting atom of
uranium can escape completely from the machine into the air. This of
course tends to break the chain. An obvious remedy to this loss of neutrons
is to make the uranium mass larger and larger. The greater the lump of
uranium, the smaller the chance of neutrons escaping. Thus, the primary
objective in making an atomic-energy machine using uranium is to pile
up the material until enough neutrons are prevented from escaping to make
the k factor greater than i. It is for this reason that the first atomic-energy
machine was known as a pile; it was made by piling up larger and larger
amounts of uranium.
