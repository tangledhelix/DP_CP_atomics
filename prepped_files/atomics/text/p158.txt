
The method which actually worked successfully goes back to a fact which
was closely associated with the discovery of the neutron itself. You will
recall that paraffin placed in the path of the mysterious neutron "radiation"
stopped it more effectively than lead and other heavy metals. It was this
property of the light atoms of carbon and hydrogen in the paraffin which
was the key to Chadwick's discovery of the neutron.

A neutron striking a heavy atom such as lead will bounce off with little
change in speed, in much the same manner that a rubber ball bounces off a
heavy wall. But, if the neutron were to strike head on with a hydrogen
nucleus of equal weight, it would lose all its speed. At the same time it
would impart its original speed to the hydrogen nucleus. Hydrogen would
be the most effective material for this purpose of slowing down neutrons
because its atoms have practically the same weight as neutrons. However,
it has the unfortunate property of capturing too many neutrons and thus
stopping the chain! Therefore, it could not be used for this purpose.

A compromise was necessary. We must use an atom of the lowest possible
weight which at the same time will not be a neutron robber. Such a lightweight
atom might be called a "slow-downer." More commonly, it is referred
to as a moderator.

Since ordinary hydrogen is unsuitable as a moderator, it was found necessary
to go up the weight scale until a light atom with negligible neutron-capture
properties was found. The next possibility is the heavy hydrogen isotope
deuterium, of atomic weight 2. This atom is excellent for the purpose;
it captures a negligible number of neutrons. Going up the weight scale, we
find beryllium of weight 9 and carbon of weight 12 with low neutron-robbing
