HEAVY TARGET

PERFECT REBOUND

This apparently contradictory fact can be easily explained if it is
that the particle is a neutron. We
must return to our atomic billiards
once again to understand this. To
simulate the situation where a
neutron of atomic weight 1 strikes
a lead nucleus of atomic weight
206, imagine a small marble
thrown with high speed striking a
heavy bowling ball. The marble
unces off with practically the
same speed and the bowling ball is
scarcely affected. It is almost as if a
ball were thrown against a wall.
The wall would absorb very little
of the original energy of the ball.
Now let one marble strike another of the same size in a head-on manner. Observation
as well as simple calculation teaches us that the "bullet" marble imparts
all its energy of motion to the
"target" marble, which then moves
forward with the same speed. Since
the neutron bullet has almost the
same mass as the proton target, the
result of a head-on collision would
be the complete transfer of energy
from the neutron to the proton.
The neutron therefore rips the
proton away from its original location
in the paraffin, and it shoots
into the detecting ionization chamber,
causing ionization of the surrounding
air. This is what had happened
to paraffin, which consists of carbon and hydrogen atoms, with many
more hydrogen than carbon atoms.

LIGHT TARGET
(EQUAL WEIGHT)

DEAD STOP!

Of course, all collisions are not head on. In the majority of cases the impact
is at an angle, which results in a sharing of the energy. In the case of the
collision of a neutron with a heavy lead nucleus at a glancing angle, the neutron
would bounce off the heavy lead atom at almost the same speed as before.
