
Chapter Eighteen

Using Electromagnets

When the problem of separating U235 on a large scale became
very pressing, every known method of isotope separation was
examined thoroughly. One of these methods, using electromagnets,
had long been familiar in the form of the mass spectrometer used in the detection
and measurement of isotopes.

In our first contact with the subject of isotopes in Chapter Seven, we showed
how the ability of a magnet to cause charged particles to swerve was used in
separating and detecting the isotopes in a sample of a gas. The material to be
tested is placed into a chamber where charged ions are produced. These are
then focused into a narrow beam and shot through a hole into a region acted
upon by a powerful magnet. The charged ions in the beam swerve in a circle
under the influence of the magnet, with the lighter atoms swerving more
sharply. The single beam thus splits into separate beams, one for each isotope
of different weight present in the material.

Originally this method of separation was used mainly to detect and measure
the isotopes of various elements. The ions in that case were arranged to strike
a photographic film which, when developed, showed the presence of each
beam of ions as a black spot (or line). The amount of the isotope could then
be determined by the amount of blackening of the film. Or an electrical
measuring instrument could be substituted for the film.
