Chapter Twenty-four

Tagging the Atom

on august 2, 1946, the Isotopes Branch of the Manhattan (Atomic
Bomb) Project sold a pinch of white powder to a medical research
institute. This sample contained a speck of a substance that will
prove of the greatest value to mankind, a radioactive twin of ordinary carbon,
called carbon 14. This isotope of carbon will be used to gather more information
about the causes and development of the dread disease, cancer.

The delivery of this small sample of carbon 14 is now being followed by
the sale of a wide variety of other radioactive atoms that will be used to solve
an almost unlimited number of pressing problems. At the same time, other
plants throughout the country are producing small quantities of stable chemical
twins in enriched form, such as heavy hydrogen, heavy carbon (carbon 13), etc.
Some of these stable isotopes will be produced by methods that were perfected
in the course of the atomic-bomb research. Many of us confidently expect
that the development of these isotopes will have useful results comparable to
the benefits of the uranium-carbon pile.
