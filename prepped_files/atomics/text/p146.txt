OFFSPRING OF URANIUM FISSION

Is it any wonder then that so
many false leads wcre investigated
between 1934 and 1939? Or that competent
investigators reported products
having varying radioactive
properties? By 1941, over 20 of these
fission fragments had been hunted
down. Among these were elements
familiar to us, such as bromine, silver,
antimony, and iodine. The skill
of the experimenters can best be appreciated
when we consider the fact
that the amounts of material studied
were so small as to be unweighable by the most delicate balances.

Uranium 235 is responsible for fission . . .

A sample of uranium, in common with so many other elements, consists
of a mixture of isotopes. With the help of the mass spectrograph, it was
known that there are at least three
isotopes of uranium existing in nature.
Of these, by far the most common
was uranium 238. If you pick
up 1,000,000 uranium atoms at random
from a sample found in nature,
about 993,000 will be U238
about 7,000 will be U235, and about
60 will be U234. In our investigations
leading to large-scale atomic
energy we shall find U238 and U235
of the utmost importance.
