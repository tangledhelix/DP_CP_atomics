What happens to a lump of sugar or a piece of cheese as it is broken into
tiny bits hardly visible to the eye and
then into particles that are indefinitely
smaller? Is matter continuous?
Or do we reach at last some
small basic particles from which the
grains of sugar or cheese are actually
built?

The keen-witted Greeks were
quick to ask these questions. One
large group of philosophers reasoned
that matter was continuous and
could be divided indefinitely without ever reaching a final building block. According
to this view, any tiny bit of matter could always be cut in half and
then cut again and again, ad infinitum.

Democritus, about 400 b.c., made the shrewd guess that everything was
made up of small particles (or atoms) that could not be divided further. This
was in no sense a scientific theory since at that time there was no way of
showing the truth or falsity of his assumption. Unfortunately, the Greeks
were not sufficiently concerned with verifying their theories by carefully
planned experiments. As a result, the conclusions of Democritus were soon
buried under a blanket of mysticism, alchemy, and fraud for almost 2,000
years.

Despite the crudeness of this ancient picture of the atom as compared with
the modern view, we must give Democritus and his disciples credit for their
boldness in presenting an idea which apparently contradicted the sense of sight.
We can see all materials as continuous. No microscope can even detect these
particles we talk about so glibly. Yet you would have to hunt a long time to
find a scientist today who would deny the existence of these tiny particles of
matter. All the evidence of the past few hundred years of research points to
their existence.

There are basic elements in all matter . . .

Aristotle, often called the father of natural science and philosophy, tried
to explain the structure of matter and also the changes that take place when
various materials are brought together. Thus wood burns and produces smoke,
ash, and heat. Water is found in many solid materials. Metals rust and change
their appearance.
