Half a billion electron volts . . .

On the relative atomic weight scale of the chemist, hydrogen atoms weighed
1.0081. Four of these coming together would weigh 4 X 1.0081, or 4.0324.
4 protons did actually join together (with 2 electrons) to form a helium nucleus,
then the total weight should be at least this figure. But the helium nucleus
weighs less than this, namely, 4.0040. Millikan therefore projected the possibility
that this process of joining together of 4 protons to form a helium
nucleus actually takes place on the stars in the tremendous commotion of
particles rushing about with enormous speeds at temperatures of many millions
of degrees. But in the process of coming together some of the mass is
lost and is converted into a splash of enormously high energy radiation.

We have already seen how Einstein's simple formula, E = M X C2, can be
used to calculate how much energy is set free when a certain amount of mass
is annihilated. Using similar calculations, Millikan computed that if heavier
atoms such as iron were to be formed from protons (hydrogen nuclei), then
the radiation produced (photons) should have energies of almost half a billion
electron volts.

This appeared too much to swallow! Half-billion electron volt energies had
hardly been imagined before. On this point, however, Millikan was shown to
be quite conservative. It is commonly agreed today that there are photons
present in the cosmic rays which have energies ranging up to 10 billion electron
volts. But did cosmic radiation consist exclusively of photons as claimed by
Millikan? Why not charged particles--electrons, protons, alphas? Further
more, what evidence was there that heavy atoms are formed from hydrogen?
Wasn't it highly improbable that 27 hydrogen atoms should come together at
the same instant and form an aluminum atom?

Now the hunt was on in real earnest, for people had begun to take sides.
Some leading physicists concurred with Millikan's view that the primary (or
"original") cosmic rays that descend upon our earth from distant spaces are
uncharged high-energy photons similar to gamma rays. Many other physicists
sided with Arthur H. Compton of the University of Chicago (also an American
Nobel prize winner), who believed that the primary cosmic radiation
consisted of high-energy electrified particles. How answer the question?
