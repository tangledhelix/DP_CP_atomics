
Other evidence from fluorescence . . .

We have mentioned earlier, in connection with Roentgen's discovery of the
x-ray, that certain minerals glowed or "fluoresced" when illuminated by the
x-rays. The glass walls of the tube also fluoresced with a greenish light when
the x-rays were produced. Thus the high-frequency x-rays were creating lower
frequency visible light in the glass and other minerals. It was found that ultraviolet
light, higher in frequency than ordinary visible light, could also cause
certain minerals to glow with lower frequency visible light.

In the fluorescent lamp this fact is utilized to multiply the visible light
output considerably. The fluorescent lamp is simply a modified cathode-ray
tube with mercury vapor in it. It creates some bluish colored visible light but
mostly invisible ultraviolet light, suitable for photographs but not for ordinary
vision. By coating the inside of the tube with a fluorescent mineral, the ultraviolet
light is converted into lower frequency visible light and the efficiency
of the lamp is greatly increased.

Another source of confirmation of the photon theory is the manner in
which x-rays themselves are created. You will recall that Roentgen discovered
the x-ray while experimenting with a cathode-ray tube in which electrons
were shooting out of the negative terminal toward the positive. It was found
that the source of the x-rays in the cathode-ray tube was the impact of the
cathode rays or electrons as they struck the positive plate in the tube. As the
fast-moving electrons bombarded the atoms of the metal on the positive plate,
they caused x-ray photons to come shooting out. It was found that the greater
the speed of the electrons, the greater was the energy of the x-ray photons
produced. This shows up in the production of "harder" or higher frequency
x-rays. Thus higher energy electron bullets produce higher energy, higher
frequency x-ray photon bullets.

This fact has received valuable application in the production of highly penetrating
x-rays. The greater the speed with which the electron bullets crash
into the positive plate of a cathode-ray tube, the greater is the energy of the
x-ray photon ejected--the more penetrating the radiation. The objective
in producing penetrating x-rays is thus to achieve the maximum speeding up
of electrons by the use of high voltages. We shall see later how the electron
speed-up process was used in the betatron to obtain x-rays that could penetrate
12 inches of steel.

Thus were forged new links in the chains that bind matter and energy together
inside the atom. Out of the study of the x-rays and visible light came
important conclusions about the behavior of electrons inside the atom. But
the most important conclusions were yet to come.
