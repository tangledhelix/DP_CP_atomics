If we could get the technical know-how of making bombs and actually
produce them in 5 years, it seems fairly likely that other nations can do the
same in an equivalent period. Whether these nations proceed to manufacture
plutonium and U235 for bombs depends to a fair extent upon whether or not
agreement can be reached within the next few years upon methods of international
control of atomic-bomb material.

Prof. Eugene P. Wigner, leading atomic physicist from Princeton University,
has pointed out that the secrets of the bomb consist in certain physical constants
used in making the bombs, a few methods of calculation, and the technical
details of our installations. Speaking in a broader sense, Dr. Oppenheimer
has pointed out the obvious physical fact that neither the nature of the world
nor of atoms can be kept a secret.

According to H. C. Urey, it will not take many years for other nations to
have atomic bombs: "Most scientific and technical men who helped produce
the bombs guess between five and ten years; a few think less and some think
more. Drs. Seitz and Bethe give convincing reasons for their estimate of
six years or less."

Can we keep ahead of other nations? . . .

H. C. Urey gives good reason to believe that we cannot.

"Some have suggested that the United States would be safe if it were to
keep ahead of other countries in the development and production of atomic
weapons. It is not certain that this country could succeed in this attempt over
any great length of time and certainly not for all time, for others would surely
come abreast of us--and perhaps in less time than we think. But let us look
more closely at this suggestion. Supposing that the United States remains ahead
of other countries in number or effectiveness of bombs, what good would it do
us? Do we plan to attack other countries at a favorable moment? Following
such an attack it would be necessary to occupy the countries with our armies
in order to prevent the manufacture of bombs in the future. Some 7 percent of
the world's population would have to keep its feet on the necks of the rest
of the world's peoples. It does not seem likely that we would choose this role
voluntarily, with a full understanding of its responsibility and hardships let
alone other considerations.
