But he found that the frog's leg was not needed at all for the creation of
this electric current. In fact, he could produce electric current merely by using
two different metals and a salt solution. By 1800 he had invented the first
electric battery. This consisted of a pile of alternate zinc and copper plates

separated in groups of two by some blotting paper soaked in salt solution.
Later he used cups of salt solution into which were dipped strips of zinc and
copper, connected together as shown in the diagram. Such a battery has sufficient
strength to light a lamp or ring a bell continuously.

Volta's discovery set the scientific world buzzing with excitement. Now it
was possible to obtain large sustained electric currents from chemicals, where
formerly only intermittent sparks of electricity could be obtained from friction.
