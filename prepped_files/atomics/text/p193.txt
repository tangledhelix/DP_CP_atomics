
Boosting production with temperature differences . . .

It was found that even slight enrichment of U235 increased the yield
of electromagnetic separators in a favorable manner. Thus, if an electromagnetic
separator could produce 1 gram of 40 per cent pure U235 in one day,
its yield would increase to twice as much (2 grams) and twice as pure (80 per
cent instead of 40 per cent) by starting with doubly enriched uranium (14
per cent instead of 0.7 per cent). Hence it was found profitable to use another
isotope separation method, the thermal diffusion process, in order to obtain a
slightly enriched sample and then to feed the latter into the electromagnetic
separators.

In an elementary form, a hot pipe or wire is run vertically down the center
of a cylindrical container filled with a gas of the material to be separated. The
outside of the container is kept at as low a temperature as feasible. The difference
in temperature causes the gas to circulate about in much the same manner
that air is heated and circulated by a radiator in a room. The lighter gases
tend to concentrate in one portion of the container and the heavier gases in
another part. Thus we can effect some separation of U235 from U238 in this
way.

The process takes a long time and requires a great deal of heat. In addition
it only produces slightly enriched uranium instead of pure uranium 235. It is
therefore not practical to use it alone. But in combination with the electromagnetic
method it can be used to supply enriched uranium and thus multiply
the production of U235 from this source. For that purpose a large-scale
plant was erected in the latter part of 1944 and was operated in conjunction
with the electromagnetic plant.

Other separation methods are possible . . .

Although the gas diffusion, electromagnetic, and thermal diffusion methods
were the major processes employed in practical large-scale separation of U235
from uranium, these are not the only conceivable methods. Others are possible.
One of these, operating on the centrifuge principle, was carried through the
test-plant stage but dropped in favor of the methods mentioned above.
