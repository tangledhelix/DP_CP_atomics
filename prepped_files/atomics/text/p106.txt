"Ghosts" in the cloud chamber . . .

It was not long before a whole series of similar transmutations were discovered.
The most effective of these producers of neutrons seem to be the
light metals, with beryllium the most efficient of all.

Further evidence for the existence of these neutrons came when nitrogen
gas was bombarded with the "radiations" resulting from the collision of alpha
particles with beryllium. The neutrons produced by these bombardments
were permitted to pass through nitrogen gas in a cloud chamber. No path
was detected for the neutrons, inasmuch as they passed between the atoms
without causing any ionization. However, the cloud tracks showed the
sudden appearance in the cloud chamber of fast ionizing particles that
seemed to have no direct connection with the source of the neutrons. What
had happened? The original path of the neutron could not be seen because
it left no ionization in its wake. But occasionally it would make a head-on
collision with a molecule and cause several ions to shoot out at great
speed from the scene of the collision. These ions have enough energy to leave
a wake of other ions in their path and produce a track in the cloud chamber.
The neutrons thus act like "ghosts" in the cloud chamber, not leaving any
tracks of their own but causing the formation of high-speed ions as shown
by the intense proton track in the illustration.
