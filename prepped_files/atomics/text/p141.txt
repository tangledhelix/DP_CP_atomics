All the experience of scientists in the field of nuclear physics led them to
expect products close to uranium. Consequently, they felt forced to look
for elements close to uranium that resembled barium and lanthanum in
chemical properties.

ATOMIC ROGUES GALLERY

A study of the family relations among elements led them to the guess that
the barium-like product was radium, element 88, and that the lanthanum-like
product was actinium, number 89. They reasoned that radium was formed
by neutron bombardment of uranium. Then radium would emit a beta particle
and would produce an atom of actinium, next higher element in the chemists'
rogues' gallery. But this picture did not appear satisfactory to many physicists.
