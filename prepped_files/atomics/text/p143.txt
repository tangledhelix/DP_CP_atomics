News of the discovery of Hahn and Strassman was first transmitted to
the United States in a telegram to Professor Niels Bohr, the renowned Danish
Nobel prize winner, in January, 1939. At that time, he was carrying out
investigations at the Institute for Advanced Study at Princeton University. On
the very day that he received the message from Europe, he was attending a
nuclear physics meeting in Washington. The scene resulting from his announcement
of uranium fission resembled the White House press room after
an important presidential declaration.

Rapid confirmation of high fission energy . . .

It was clear that the large energy release expected to accompany fission
should readily be detected by equipment in the laboratory of every nuclear
physicist. Six independent confirmations of this discovery were reported in
the February, 1939, issue of a leading scientific publication, the Physical
Review. Within a few months, the high energies resulting from uranium
fission had been reported in the leading American, British, German, and
French scientific journals.
