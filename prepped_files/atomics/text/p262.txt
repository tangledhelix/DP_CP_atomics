The great hope of our cancer radiologists is that a method of administering
neutron bombardment of body cells will be found that is preferentially more
destructive of cancerous tissue than of ordinary healthy tissue. It is an
empirical attack, not seeking the causes of this blight nor completely attacking
its origins.

Since 1938 fast neutrons have been explored for this purpose. About 230
patients thought to have incurable cancer were treated with fast neutrons fr
September, 1939, to February, 1943. In June, 1945, 26 were still alive. The
general conclusion from these and other studies was that fast neutron therapy
showed hopeful possibilities but that considerable work remained to be done
before a definite answer could be given. Physicians who have achieved success
in individual cases are not prone to bring false hopes to millions of suffering
men and women. Yet the possibilities are so promising that generous
support has been enlisted from many scientific foundations and philanthropic
agencies.

Ingenious use of slow neutrons . . .

During the past few years, a novel method of attack on cancerous tissue
has been evolved. The entire approach has been made possible by the closest
teamwork between the nuclear physicists--once considered to be among the
loftiest dwellers in "ivory towers"--and the physicians. We have already seen
how slow neutrons can be produced by allowing fast neutrons to make repeated
collisions with light atoms, such as hydrogen and carbon. In fact,
the use of these atoms as moderators made possible the first atomic-energy
pile. Despite the fact that slow neutrons have such little energy, they encounter
only slight difficulty in falling into and disrupting the nuclei of target
atoms. High-energy particles ejected from the target atom can then be used
in treatment.

The essential idea suggested by Dr. P. G. Kruger of the University of Illinois
is this : Let us assume that some boron or lithium compound could be placed
near or actually inside some cancerous tissue and the whole region containing
this tissue irradiated with slow neutrons. Now, these neutrons will have litdc
effect on the region as a whole. But the collisions of these slow neutrons with
the boron or lithium atoms in the cancerous tissue would cause the ejection
of alpha particles with energies of 1 M.e.v. These high-energy alphas would
then cause destruction of tissue only in the immediate vicinity of the boron
or lithium atom. In this way the indiscriminate destruction of tissue over a
