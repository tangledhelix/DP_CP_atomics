
"We are forced to recognize that the composition of true compounds is as
invariable as their properties; between pole and pole they are identical in these
two respects. The cinnabar of Japan has the same properties and composition
as that of Spain : silver chloride is identically the same, whether obtained from
Peru or Siberia...."

We shall soon see how simple and yet how important a point this was.

More clues demanded an explanation . . .

Using scales that could detect a change in weight of less than 1/10,000 ounce,
these pioneer investigators found that there was no loss or gain in weight as a
result of a chemical change. Entirely different materials were formed, yet
the total weights before and after appeared to be the same.

Thus when i pound of hydrogen and 8 pounds of oxygen are placed in a
strong container and ignited, exactly 9 pounds of water are formed. When
23 pounds of the soft silvery metal sodium combine chemically with 35H
pounds of the greenish-yellow poisonous gas chlorine, the end result is 58-1/2
pounds of common harmless table salt. Twice as much sodium requires exactly
twice as much chlorine. If there are 23 pounds of sodium and 36 pounds
of chlorine, then only 35-1/2 pounds of chlorine will be used up and 1/2 pound
will remain unused. If we attempt to combine 24 pounds of sodium with 35-1/2
pounds of chlorine, we find that only 23 pounds of sodium are used up and
converted into table salt, leaving behind 1 pound of unused sodium.

Observe that these changes produce materials which in no way resemble
the original constituents. Sodium causes violent spattering when placed on
water and produces serious burns to flesh by mere contact. Chlorine was
actually the first deadly poison gas of modern warfare. Yet their chemical
union produces harmless table salt. Similarly, the two gases hydrogen and
oxygen can be exploded to form the familiar product water.

A Quaker schoolmaster puts all the clues together . . .

Why is the proportion of sodium to chlorine in table salt always 23:35-1/2?
Why is the proportion of oxygen to hydrogen in water always 8:1, whether
the water is obtained from rain, from the ocean, or from ice? Why should
the weights of the products formed from a chemical change always equal the
weights of the original materials?
