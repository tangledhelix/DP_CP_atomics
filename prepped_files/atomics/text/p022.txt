The investigations of the nineteenth century scientists provided the answer
to the question about the nature of heat. Heat is simply the fynetic energy of
the motion of molecules.

If you heat a gas the molecules move more rapidly. If the molecules are
in a closed container and the gas is heated, then the greater motion of the
molecules will exert a greater pressure on the walls.

What about solids and liquids, in which the molecules are not as free to
move around as they are in a gas? In that case the molecules are in rapid to-and-fro
vibrational motion in the manner of Davy's "corpuscular" vibrations.

We now have an explanation for the chaotic zigzag motions of the small
particles as observed by the botanist Brown. The water molecules were, of
course, too small to be seen. But the particles suspended in water were subjected
to collisions with these water molecules moving violently about. The
suspended particles were then buffeted about like toy sailboats in a storm.

Energy was also conserved . . .

Out of the study of heat came important conclusions about the nature of
energy that proved to be of fundamental importance in atomic science. It was
discovered that a definite quantity of heat energy was equal to a fixed quantity
of mechanical energy, or motion. This was an early step in the discovery of
the law of conservation of energy, the companion to the chemist's law of conservation
of matter (or mass). Just as the chemist found that matter could not
be created or destroyed but could only be transformed into different forms,
so the physicist discovered that energy could not be created or destroyed but
only transformed into different forms.

It remained for Einstein in our present century to combine these two principles
into one in which both matter and energy are shown to be equivalent
and convertible into each other.

Dramatic illustration of the conversion of matter into energy has been
furnished on this earth by the setting off of a number of atomic bombs. In a
rather humdrum but vital manner, the sun and the stars have been transforming
matter into energy for billions of years. We shall return to this
fundamental topic in a later chapter.
