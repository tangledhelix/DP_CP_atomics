more potent drugs, more effective insecticides, the secret of body growth and

atomic picture. The important task is to join various atoms together to form
molecules of new compounds. The everyday chemist considers the atoms of
the elements as beads, and his task is to string them together in the right manner.

Take one of the simplest of all chemical processes, the burning of coal.
What is taking place? Carbon atoms from the coal join with oxygen atoms
present in the air. In this change molecules of carbon dioxide (CO2) are

formed and heat is liberated. We refer to this heat as chemical energy mainly
because it results from the "restringing of the beads" and not from the breaking
up of the beads themselves.

A similar process of chemical joining together of atoms into molecules enables
trees and other plant life to build up molecules of starch and sugar (containing
carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen) from carbon dioxide in the air and
water from the ground. With the addition of nitrates and other minerals from
the ground, plants then convert sugar into complex proteins, of which the
individual molecules may contain thousands of atoms each. We, in turn, eat
these plants and convert their plant protein molecules into more suitable arrangements
which enable us to continue the processes of life.
