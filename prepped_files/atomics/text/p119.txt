Particle or photon? . . .

For the physicist, however, the basic question was, Are the primary cosmic
radiations that are coming to us from interstellar space high-speed electrically
charged particles or high-energy photons similar to gamma rays? It was
really not an easy question to answer.

We are fairly certain that the radiations that we can detect are of two kinds.
There are high-energy gamma-ray photon radiations that we can detect with
our instruments. There are also high-energy charged particles. Which came
first from outer space? If the photons came from outer space (and were therefore
primary) then they could smash apart atoms in the air and eject extremely
high energy particles such as electrons, protons, and neutrons. These would
be secondary particles and would be detected by our instruments.

On the other hand, suppose high-speed particles were the primary cosmic
radiations. On entering our atmosphere they could collide with atoms and
eject high-energy particles and also gamma-ray photons. These would be secondary
gammas, however. At the risk of using a hackneyed example, we might
venture to say that some of the heated debate on this subject resembled the
question of the prior existence of the chicken or the egg.

It was soon established fairly definitely that there were high-speed charged
particles in the cosmic radiation. This was shown in a scries of beautiful
experiments that illustrate the cooperative nature of science on a world scale.
In 1931, Arthur Compton organized a world survey to take simultaneous
measurements from about 70 different stations located all over the earth.
