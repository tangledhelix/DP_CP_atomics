Chapter Four

Atomic Secrets from Empty Tubes

What can an empty tube tell us about the nature of the atom? One
would suppose this to be a rather idle question. But like other
surprises in scientific sleuthing, an empty tube was to provide
basic clues about the structure of the atom. Furthermore, offspring of this
empty tube were to become fundamentally important devices in radio, television,
lighting, and radar. How did this come about?
It all started with an observation
made by the Frenchman Masson
in 1853. Instead of making electric
sparks pass through the air, why
not see what happens when a spark
is sent through a tube from which
some of the air has been pumped
out? He sealed two wires from the
terminals of a sparking machine
into the opposite ends of a closed glass tube. When some of the air was pumped
out, the spark jumped a greater distance.
