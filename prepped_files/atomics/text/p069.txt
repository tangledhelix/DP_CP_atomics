PHOTON

ENERGY LEVEL

According to Bohr, electrons could travel around the nucleus only in a set
of definite orbits. Electrons in an outer orbit have greater energies than
electrons in an orbit closer to the nucleus, but every orbit corresponds to a
definite energy. As a crude analogy, consider a many-laned race track,
horse in the innermost lane must move at, say, 5 miles per hour. In the next
lane, a horse must move at 6 miles per hour, etc. Popular atom models usually
show these "Bohr orbits" about the nucleus. Another useful model arranges the
various orbits as steps on an "energy-level staircase" as shown in the illustration.
The lowest step corresponds to the orbit closest to the nucleus. An electron
on a higher step has a greater energy than one on a lower step. The illustration
shows an electron j umping from the fourth to the first step. As a result,
