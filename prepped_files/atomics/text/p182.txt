A heavy-water pile and a plutonium-separation plant have recently been
completed and are now operating at Chalk River, Ontario. The size of the
effort here may be gauged by the 20-million-dollar capital outlay and the
3-1.2-million-dollar annual operating budget for the plant.

A major factor in the peacetime use of atomic energy is the consideration
of compactness. Another important factor is the need for a good coolant inside
the pile. Both these important considerations make heavy water a desirable
moderator material. The use of heavy water in piles is now limited by the
expense of its production and its lack of availability. In the future, as peacetime
uses of atomic energy are developed, heavy water may well take its
proper place along with carbon as a moderator in the uranium pile.
