detail because in practically no case were the scientists on the atomic-bomb
project given the luxury of being
allowed to proceed in this cautious
manner. This was certainly true
of the plutonium extraction process.
But there was little choice in
the matter. Of what avail would
it be to have great quantities of
plutonium present in hot uranium
slugs if no large-scale plant were
ready to extract this precious ingredient?

The plutonium-separation plant
at Clinton, Tenn., was designed on the basis of experiments that used only
microgram quantities of plutonium. A gram is 1/28 ounce. A microgram is
one-millionth of a gram and corresponds to approximately 55 billionths of an
ouncel The air in a human breath weighs about 250,000 micrograms, hundreds
of times heavier than the amounts of plutonium available for study in the
early stages.

By the end of 1942, about 500 micrograms of pure plutonium salts were
available. This amount would not be enough to cover the head of a pin. Yet
it was necessary, using this speck of material, to study chemical methods of
separating pure plutonium from a great many elements. And it was upon
this information that an enormous plant was to be erected. The task seems
more amazing when it is realized that only 18 months earlier absolutely
nothing was known about this element.
