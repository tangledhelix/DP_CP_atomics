
Chapter Three

Other Roads Lead to the Atom

We have seen how the nature of matter was probed by the pioneers
in chemistry. They investigated the changes that took place in
materials around us, the burning of wood and its conversion
into ash and smoke, the refining of shiny metal from its native ore. These
chemists approached the problem from the point of view of chemical changes.
Another group of inquisitive people, the physicists, asked different questions
about nature. What is heat? What is electricity? What is light? The
answers that their investigations provided also built up giant industries which
today include automobiles, airplanes, radios, electrical appliances, and all our
modern means of transportation and communication.

Yet, despite their different approach to the problems, their researches also
led inexorably to atoms and molecules as basic particles of matter.

The physicist deals with energy . . .

Most of the questions which the physicist asks about nature can be classified
broadly under the heading of energy. Here is a word that is widely used but
little understood. We are urged to eat "energy cereals" for breakfast. We
hear about heat energy, electric energy, radiant energy, high-energy particles,
and now atomic energy. What is the meaning of this much-used and much-abused
word?
