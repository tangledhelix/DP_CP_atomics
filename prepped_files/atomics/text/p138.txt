It is necessary to shoot millions of bullets before you can expect to get one
hit. Before atomic energy could become a practical reality, a way had to be
found to increase the likelihood that our bullets would be effective.

And here is where the neutron provided the key with which large-scale
atomic energy could be realized.

Looking for elements beyond uranium . . .

In 1934, a relatively innocent experiment by the young Italian physicist
Fermi was to lead by a winding path to the discovery of uranium "fission"
and thus open up the Atomic Age. The clues that were uncovered were numerous
and many led into blind alleys. An international hunt for the
explanation to many puzzling observations was to continue until 1939. The
answer to these puzzles was to mark a turning point in our world civilization
far more significant to all of us than the discovery of fire, of the steam engine,
and of electricity.

At the University of Rome, Fermi and his vigorous research team were
embarked on a systematic hunting expedition. Neutrons were used as artillery
and atoms were the targets. Hunting was unusually productive. Neutrons, not
having any charge, readily penetrated through the electron shells surrounding
the nuclei of target atoms and brought about interesting changes.

For example, consider an atom of iron as the target. The nucleus has a
charge of 26+. When a neutron strikes this nucleus it is absorbed, resulting
in an isotope of iron, with weight increased by 1. But this isotope is unstable
and a beta particle, or high-speed electron, is shot out from the nucleus. The
loss of this negative charge leaves the nucleus with a greater positive charge
than before. Instead of a charge of 26+ the nucleus now has 27+. The net
result of the addition of a neutron is therefore to produce an element having
one higher atomic number. In this case, the product is cobalt, atomic number
27. In the same way, gold (atomic number 79) could be made from platinum
(atomic number 78), and mercury (atomic number 80) from gold. Of
course, these reactions would not be profitable according to current market
prices for these metals!
