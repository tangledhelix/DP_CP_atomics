Thus the growth factor of atom smashing is the most important part of
the process. The point of balance between a chain reaction that is successful
and one that is a dud is a hair line. With the growth factor less than
1--ever so slightly--then the original neutrons do not smash apart enough
atoms to replace themselves and in a fraction of a second the process stops
and the release of atomic energy ceases. On the other hand, if the growth
factor is maintained at greater than 1--ever so slightly--then the uranium
atoms "fiss" in ever-increasing numbers until the material explodes. To
control the release of atomic energy gradually for peacetime uses requires
that the k factor be almost exactly 1 at all times. In other words, a k number
just slightly more than 1 must be achieved at the start so that the process
accelerates to the point we wish to reach with the release of the required
