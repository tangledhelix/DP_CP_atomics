
Chapter Ten

Finding the Neutron

The neutron--conjecture at first, today a reality . . .

In an earlier chapter we were invited to play a rather unusual game--building
up the nuclei of atoms. We were given two building blocks
for the purpose. One was already quite familiar to us, the proton. The
other was introduced to us rather hurriedly so that we might get on with the
game. This was the neutron, a particle having about the same mass as the
proton but without any electric charge at all. We would expect to find this
particle with considerably different properties from the proton and electron
because it has no charge, and therefore it is not swerved by electric or magnetic
forces. In the decade and a half since its discovery, it has been found to play
a most significant role in the realm of atomic projectiles and a basic part in the
liberation of large-scale atomic energy. How was this strange new particle
discovered? What are its unusual properties?
