Gamma rays, penetrating rays akin to x-rays, are also present . . .

In addition to the alpha and beta particles, a third form of radiation was
found emanating from naturally radioactive material. Some of the radiation
was completely unaffected by electric or magnetic forces and traveled in
straight lines, although alphas and betas were deflected by these forces. It was
therefore concluded that this radiation consisted of photon bullets similar to
x-rays. Furthermore, like x-rays, this radiation was able to penetrate great
thicknesses of matter. Today, we refer to this radiation as gamma rays.

The direct paths of these photon bullets from gamma rays are invisible in
the cloud chamber. However, they ionize the air in the same manner as do the
x-rays. They knock but a large number of electrons from the molecules which
they strike. It is the paths of these electrons which are observed in the Wilson
cloud chamber. Since these electrons have considerably lower energy than
the gamma-ray photons, their paths are short and crooked, as seen in the
illustration.

Gamma rays have approximately ioo times the penetrating power of beta
particles and 10,000 times that of alpha particles. Some of the rays emitted by
radioactive materials are "soft," low-frequencey gammas which are stopped
by about 1/2 inch of lead. "Hard," high-frequency gamma rays produced by
some radioactive atoms can penetrate several inches of lead.
