potentialities of uranium for fuel-replacement purposes from such a comparison.

Let us take amounts of fuel that will produce equal amounts of heat.
Starting at the freezing temperature, i pound of U235 could supply enough
heat to boil 9,500 tons of water; 140 pounds of ordinary uranium (which contains
1 pound of U235) would also produce this same amount of heat.
would require about 1,500 tons of coal and 330,000 gallons of fuel oil or gasoline
to heat this same quantity of water.

The cost of this quantity of coal (at $7 per ton) would be $10,500. The cost
of fuel oil (at 4 cents a gallon) would be $13,500, while the cost of gasoline
(at 15 cents a gallon) would be $50,000. But ordinary uranium ore was selling
before the war for $2 per pound. At this price the cost of 140 pounds would
only be $280. Even assuming that purifying costs raised this to $10 per pound,
uranium fuel would still be considerably cheaper than the equivalent amount
of coal.

Omitting all other considerations, ordinary uranium, usable in a uranium-graphite
chain-reacting pile, could thus compete most favorably on the basis
of the cost of the fuel. We do not have any estimates of the cost of U235.
However, it requires $10,500 worth of coal to produce the same amount of
heat as a pound of U235. It may therefore be possible for pure U235 to compete
with coal even at a price of thousands of dollars per pound.

Between the two extremes of ordinary uranium and pure U235, we have a
whole range of prices for enriched uranium with various proportions of U235.
A price of $100 per pound for an enriched uranium containing 20 per cent
U235 would still be one-twentieth the cost of coal. In fact, we could afford to
go up to a price of $2,000 per pound for this enriched uranium and still compete
with the fuel cost of coal.

coal, oil, or gasoline depends mainly on the manner in which atomic energy
can be adapted to do the jobs that are performed by our present fuels. Can
it be used to heat homes, generate electricity, drive trains, ships, cars, planes?
