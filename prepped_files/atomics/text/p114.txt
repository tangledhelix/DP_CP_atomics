It was because of a puzzling escape of electricity from a simple instrument,
the electroscope, that basic problems were raised. The story of the discoveries
arising from the investigation of an apparently insignificant leakage of electric
charge is one to delight the fancies of the most exacting detective-story
fan.

A simple electroscope and mysterious radiation . . .

Ever since scientific investigations in the eighteenth century, the electroscope
has proved of great value in ferreting out the secrets of electricity. The instrument
is simplicity itself.

Make two leaves by folding over a
strip of very light metal foil. Now
attach the tip of the V to the end of
a metal rod. Place the rod and leaves
in a container in order to avoid
leakage of electric charge. To operate,
place an electric charge on the
rod. The charge distributes itself in
the rod and leaves. The two leaves
now repel each other and separate
because they both have the same
kind of charge. The greater the
electric charge, the greater the separation
of the leaves. This can then
be used to measure the amount of charge. With very thin gold foil or other
light materials, extremely minute amounts of electric charge can be detected.
