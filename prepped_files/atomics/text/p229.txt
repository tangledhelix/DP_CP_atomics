The plutonium formed from U238 may be the key . . .

Every splitting of a U235 atom removes from the pile a "fissionable" atom.
But we have seen that as the pile operates plutonium is formed from the plentiful
atoms of U238. This product can also be split by neutrons, liberating large
quantities of atomic energy. We have already mentioned the use of plutonium
in atomic bombs. In other words, the formation of plutonium actually provides
"additional fuel" for the atomic power plant and makes it possible to
extend the life of a pile. Similar considerations apply to the use of thorium,
from which is produced the fissionable material U233.

But after a sufficient time, the loss of fissionable material may bring the
chain reaction to a halt before all the U238 and thorium are used up. What can
be done to "stoke this fire"? Some U235, manufactured in the gas diffusion
or electromagnetic separation plants that we have described, can be added to
the pile and it will again be chain reacting. A second solution is to add some
plutonium which has been accumulated by the operation of other piles. Both
methods allow more and more of the U238 and thorium atoms to become
useful as power sources.

Special advantages of atomic-energy piles . . .

In addition to the factor of reduced cost, there are other considerations which
make the use of atomic energy desirable for certain purposes. Perhaps the
most important of these considerations is the compactness of the energy source.
It will not be necessary to be continually supplying large amounts of fuel to
the engine. This might be especially desirable for the setting up of mining and
chemical operations in remote sites. Occasionally the desired ore is located
far from sources of the energy needed to extract the metal from the raw
material. In that case, the ore is either never worked or it must be transported
great distances to the industrial centers. This is wasteful practice,
because in the case of a highly impure ore the railroad and ship are carrying
a good deal of useless "rock."

Recall that 1 pound of U235 is equivalent in energy to 3 million pounds
of coal. Once the pile is constructed at the remote site, it will continue to
produce energy for very long periods of time. When enrichment becomes
necessary the actual weight of U235 needed for this purpose is negligible compared
with the energy produced from it.
