
Large-scale production . . .

On the basis of the improvements that had been developed by the group
of scientists working with Lawrence at California, it was decided in September,
1942, to proceed with the erection of an electromagnetic separation plant
Location of the plant at Clinton, Tenn., afforded convenient access to TVA
electric power needed in large quantities for the electromagnets. The main
reason for the decision to proceed with such a plant was that it was the only
method at the time which could produce greatly enriched U235 almost immediately.
At the same time, each production unit could be relatively small
Each magnet was a unit in itself that could be used for production of enriched
U235. Unlike the gaseous diffusion method, which required thousands
of stages, the electromagnetic method needed considerably fewer stages.
Quantity in this case could be obtained by building many individual units, the
only limitation being that of expense. But at the same time it was more certain
than any other known method. Furthermore, improvements discovered at a
later date could always be introduced into new units as they were constructed.

In the actual operation of these electromagnetic separation units at Clinton,
Tenn., it was found possible to increase the yield in a number of ways. By
widening the slits and the beam, a certain amount of separation is lost and the
collectors get an enriched sample instead of pure U235. This enriched sample
can then be used over again in another electromagnetic separator for further
enrichment. It was found more economical to widen the beam and use several
stages of operation than to narrow the beam down and obtain a much smaller
quantity of pure U235 with one operation.

As a result of these procedures it was possible to obtain practical amounts
of U235 from magnets. By 1945, the electromagnetic plant had proved successful
in the attainment of the original objective, that of making U235 for
atomic bombs. According to Smyth: "The electromagnetic separation plant
was in large-scale operation during the winter of 1944-1945, and produced
U235 of sufficient purity for use in atomic bombs."

The plant for electromagnetic separation of U235 at Clinton--like the gas
diffusion plant--represented an enormous effort. It consists of 175 buildings,
erected at a cost of almost a third of a billion dollars. About 28,000 workers
were required at the peak of the construction period and 22,000 operating personnel
were required at peak production. Because of the scarcity of copper
during the war, over 14,000 tons of silver, worth more than 400 million dollars,
were borrowed from the Treasury Department to be used for cables and wires
in the plant.
