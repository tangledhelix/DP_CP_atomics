Chapter Twenty-five

Medicine in the Atomic Age

In the course of unlocking the secrets of the atom, mankind has been
given powerful tools with which to study disease and to attack some of
the worst plagues that have burdened us since time immemorial. This
particularly vital search started soon after the discovery of x-rays and the
isolation of radium. The invention of the cyclotron furnished us with powerful
sources of high-speed particles that could be applied to medicine.

The development of chain-reacting piles has now given us a twofold supply
of riches. Neutrons in hitherto undreamed-of number will be available both for
direct therapy and for the production of various man-made radioactive atoms.
With these great boons to healing stemming from the harnessing of atomic
energy, we may look forward hopefully to rewards that will outweigh the
unfortunate and destructive effects of atomic bombs.
