
The neutron is an excellent bombardier . . .

The fact that the neutron has no electric charge makes it one of the most
effective projectiles in the roll call of atomic artillery. This is so despite the
fact that we are able to speed up charged particles such as protons by applying
electric and magnetic forces, which we cannot do with neutrons. But the
electric charge on particles produced by atom guns also makes it very difficult
for them to penetrate into the nucleus. A high-speed proton or alpha particle
can penetrate the light electron rings about the nucleus quite easily, but it is
strongly repelled as it approaches the like positive charge on the nucleus. The
number of actual effective hits is therefore very small compared with the
number of particles that must be used in the bombardment.

However, the neutron does not experience this difficulty. Since it has no
electric charge, it can approach very close to the nucleus of the target atom
without being repelled and therefore scores a far greater percentage of hits.
In fact, most neutrons eventually end their existence by entering the nucleus
of some atom.

The neutron can bring about many transmutations . . .

Scarcely any element is "impregnable" to neutron bombardment. In many
cases the first step in a successful penetration is the simple capture of the
neutron by the target atom. This merely adds to the weight of the nucleus
without changing the nature of the element itself. But this chemical twin that
is formed is frequently unstable. It is radioactive and frequently ejects a beta
particle from the nucleus. However, this emission of a negative particle leaves
a nucleus with one higher positive charge. In other words, the next higher
element in the chemists' rogues' gallery, or table of elements, is produced.
In this way many striking examples of artificially radioactive materials have
been produced. (We shall leave to a later chapter the story of the discovery
of man-made radioactivity.)

Let us expose a copper coin to neutrons. Observe that the coin is now
radioactive! What has happened?

Neutron (bullet) + copper (target) --> radioactive copper

nucleus.

Radioactive copper --> zinc + beta particle
