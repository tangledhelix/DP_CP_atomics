
But the value of radioactive iodine goes beyond its use to diagnose the condition
of the thyroid gland. In the case of an overactive thyroid, it has been found
that treatment with radioactive iodine taken by mouth can cure the condition.
In 50 cases treated in this manner, success in controlling the condition was
achieved for 80 per cent of the patients. Therefore, the use of this radioactive
iodine amounts to a cure for the large majority of persons afflicted with overactive
thyroid.

Radio-iodine and thyroid cancers . . .

Some cases of cancer of the thyroid seem to be amenable to treatment with
radioactive iodine. In one case recently where surgical operation had removed
a cancerous thyroid gland, it was found that some of the cancerous tissue had
spread to other parts of the body. When radioactive iodine was given to the
patient, it was learned that these cancerous spots could be definitely located
by instruments outside the body because they were picking up the radioactive
iodine. This opens up the interesting possibility that other cancers that have
spread might be located in the same way provided the proper tracer could
be found.

But more than this, it was discovered that the radioactive iodine settling
in the cancerous spots was suppressing the growth of the cancer cells. As a
result the patient is still alive and the growth seems to be under control. Thus,
a specific radioactive element was found that could attach itself to the cancerous
tissue and keep it under control. This opens up similar possibilities for
other types of cancer.

Unfortunately, only a small percentage of cancers of the thyroid seem
amenable to this method of treatment. Nevertheless, the hope of further development
along this line of attack is bright.

Leukemia aided by the use of radioactive phosphorus . . .

Leukemia is a cancer in which the organs responsible for the formation of
blood produce too many white blood corpuscles. These overabundant white
cells are not normal and they have reduced ability to perform their regular
function of preventing infection. This disease often ends fatally. For many
years x-ray treatment has shown a beneficial effect in chronic cases. It develops
after many experiments that the use of radioactive phosphorus is at least as
beneficial as the x-ray treatment and in certain respects is sometimes more
advantageous to use.
