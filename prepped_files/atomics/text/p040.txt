
Chapter Five

Light Illuminates the Atom

The nineteenth century was a time of great and fruitful scientific
discovery, so much so that many thought the zenith of achievement
had been reached. But three rapid-fire scientific discoveries at the
very end of the century made exploration into new and uncharted fields
necessary. The solution of the questions raised by these discoveries was to lead
within a brief period of 50 years to mankind's successful control over atomic
energy.

In 1895 Roentgen discovered the x-ray. Only a year later Becquerel discovered
a mysterious radioactivity emanating from uranium. By 1898 the
Curies had isolated radium. The startling thing all three discoveries had in
common was the extremely penetrating radiation that could pass through
solid opaque objects with ease. Scientists began to study these radiations intensively.
