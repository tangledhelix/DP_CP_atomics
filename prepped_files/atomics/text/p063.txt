It is clear from these figures that the prospective purchaser of a gram of
highly prized radium need have little worry about his property disappearing
under his very eyes. However, if he purchases a quantity of the other radioactive
element discovered by Madame Curie, polonium, then at the end of 140
days only half his purchase would remain, and to add to his discomfiture he
would find that his valuable property had converted itself to the nonradioactive,
inexpensive element, lead!

Tracing radium's family tree, past and future . . .

If we consider radium as the parent element, then radon gas might be
considered a daughter element, or the second generation. Radon gas is itself
highly radioactive. It is well known that tiny capsules containing radon gas
are frequently used in "radium treatments." This daughter element, on the
average, leads a very brief existence. Its half-life is only 3.82 days. In its self-destruction,
it emits an alpha particle. If a wire is inserted into the radon gas,
a very small amount of solid material deposits on this wire. On examination,
the solid material is found to consist of several radioactive elements, each
having a definite survival chance. It is thus clear that several generations are
