The "race" starts with a small electron
gun, consisting of our familiar
hot wire evaporating electrons,
which shoots some electrons into the
tube. The magnetic force now takes
over. The current in the giant magnet
is steadily increased, creating a
stronger and stronger magnetic
force. As a result the electrons are
forced to move in perfect circles inside
the tube. Each time around the
electron picks up 400 e.v. of energy.
This does not look too promising,
until we consider that it will make a quarter of a million rotations in the doughnut
before it is released. It thus comes shooting at the target with an energy of
100 M.e.v.

How long does this process take? The entire time for the quarter million
rotations is but a brief 1/240 second. Remember that the electron has been
traveling at speeds close to that of light. At these speeds it could travel several
times around the earth in a second.

At the end of the brief fraction of a second during which the electron is in
the tube, an electrical tripping mechanism causes the electron beam to swerve
out of its circular path to strike a target of tungsten. The impact of these
100-M.e.v. bullets results in the emission of the most penetrating x-rays ever
made by man. In fact, these rays are far more penetrating than die gamma
rays from radium and approach the energies found in cosmic rays. They can
penetrate 12 inches of steel.

As soon as one cycle is completed, the current in the electromagnet is made to
reverse. At the proper moment another beam of electrons is shot into the
tube and the whirling starts again. Thus there are many separate electron
groups every second, each group whirling a quarter of a million times inside
the doughnut-shaped tube.
