As a result of this survey it was
definitely established that there is
a connection between the amount of radiation and the magnetic field of the
earth. Charged particles arc therefore indicated as one of the components of
this cosmic radiation.

The last word has not yet been written . . .

It appears at the present time that the primary cosmic rays coming from
outer space consist, in the main, of high-energy particles. It is estimated that
i billion billion of these particles enter the earth's atmosphere every second.
These high-energy particles create an enormous number of secondary particles
and gammas, which stream down upon us from above.

When we examine the radiations more closely we find that there arc two
distinct groups at sea level. There is a "soft" group of particles and gammas
that can penetrate about 4 inches of lead. Another group consists of "hard"
rays with energies of several thousand M.e.v. capable of penetrating over 40
inches of lead. Compare these with the most energetic 50-M.e.v. particles
produced by the cyclotron in the early forties.

Taking the picture of cosmic rays as we know them today, it is clear that
a great deal remains to be discovered about them. Where do they come from?
Do they, as Millikan boldly ventured to assert, really come from processes
in which atoms are being transmuted into other atoms in interstellar space?
Was there at one time a violent atomic upheaval in the universe which gave
rise to these radiations? It is possible that the cosmic radiations hitting us now
as we read this chapter started upon their travels many millions of years ago
from distant stars and galaxies. Arc these radiations the "cosmic dust," now
hitting us as a token of an atomic catastrophe long past?
