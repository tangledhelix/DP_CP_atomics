Can we duplicate this process, or something similar to it, on our earth? At
the present moment scientists do not think so. They believe that this process
can only take place in the interior of the sun at temperatures of many millions
of degrees. At these temperatures the atoms are moving about at speeds close
to that of light. Their energies are great enough to overcome the repulsive
forces existing between positive charges on the nuclei and can thus smash
into each other on a large scale rather than on the "one-in-a-million" scale
of our atom guns. Thus the secret of large-scale atomic energy on the sun is its
high interior temperature, which makes most atoms capable of smashing
through the outer guard of the nucleus.

Actually, all atoms at the lower and upper ends of the scale in the rogues'
gallery of the elements are capable of liberating energy by transformation into
the more stable elements of medium weight. The sun accomplishes this with
the lightest element. We, on the other hand, accomplish it with the heaviest
natural element. In between there is a vast storehouse waiting for whoever
can get the key with which to unlock it.

If you had asked a reputable scientist in 1930 whether large-scale atomic
energy were possible his answer would have been "probably not." Pressed
for reasons, he would have given all the rules of the atomic game known at
the time. In the end he might have said "probably not, from all known indications."
But the rules of the game have changed very rapidly since 1930.
The neutron was discovered in 1932. Man-made radioactivity came quickly
onto the scene. Atom-smashing machines began to be developed. Fission
of U235, hardly even considered as a possibility, became a reality. With the
changing rules the improbable became a possibility, and in a brief interval of
time, a reality.

Thus, we leave to the future the consideration of the question of the possibility
of extracting atomic energy from the more plentiful lighter atoms. With
the known rules it does not seem possible to achieve the million-degree temperatures
on earth that enable the stars to transmute hydrogen to helium.
