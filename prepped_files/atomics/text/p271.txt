There probably will be no effective defense . . .

The very nature of an explosively destructive missile rules out an adequate
defense once it is fired. We have no way of preventing a TNT shell from
exploding once it leaves the gun. We have no way of stopping the explosion
of an atomic bomb once it is set off.

Our efforts at defense must be directed toward destroying the gun that fires
the shell or the plane that carries the bomb. The results of the last war demonstrated
the difficulty of this task. Although a considerable degree of success was
achieved with the V-i "buzz bomb" after some time had elapsed, the far more
rapid V-2 presented an insoluble problem. Since the destructiveness of the
atomic bomb is so great, it would be sufficient for even a small number of
them to get through. The damaging effect of the Japanese "Kamikaze" suicide
corps on our shipping off Okinawa was a serious menace, despite the fact
that only ordinary explosives were carried.

Bombers of conventional design, however, will not be the real threat
Sweeping technological advances in warfare have introduced new military
concepts. These include pilotless aircraft, and jet and rocket craft. Launching
could be made from land bases, submarines, or aircraft carriers. It is not outside
the realm of reasonable thought to envisage aircraft powered by jets, rockets,
or atomic energy, carrying many tons of atomic explosives, crossing oceans
and continents at stratospheric heights in a matter of minutes. Against such
speeds and short warning time it appears that detector defenses will be of little
avail.
