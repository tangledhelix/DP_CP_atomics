A most interesting feature of this machine is that the safest place for a man
is inside the metal sphere itself! Work can go on inside this apparently dangerous
spot without any noticeable effect on the people in it. This is due to
the fact that like electric charges repel each other and always tend to travel to
the outside of metal objects. The electric charge therefore has no effect on objects
and persons inside the sphere.

In a later and less spectacular model, the two spheres were combined into
one unit for collecting the charge and one of the supporting columns was
used as the "accelerating tube" for the charged particles. In this form the
machine was more compact and convenient for atom-smashing experiments.

The Van de Graaff generator relies on simple brute force to give the charged
particles the high speeds necessary for atom smashing. Once the voltage has
been built up, a particle in the tube is given one steady but gigantic push as it
speeds to the other terminal. Another machine, designed with great ingenuity
by E. O. Lawrence of California, relies upon a series of steady, separate pushes
to achieve equivalent energies.

The cyclotron--atomic merry-go-round . . .

The basic principle of the cyclotron is analogous to the increase of speed
given to a boy on a swing. If he is given a small push every time the swing is
completed, he will go faster and faster, and higher and higher, because the
slight increases in speed caused by each push add up.

The happy feature of the cyclotron is that the high-speed particles can be
given energies of millions of electron volts by using a series of many small
pushes obtained, say, from only a 10,000-volt source. The particle is speeded
up by 300 pushes from the source to the same extent that it would be by a
3,000,000-volt Van de Graaff generator.
