The deuteron, another atom-smashing projectile . . .

Three basic particles of matter have been described--the electron, the proton,
and the neutron. Protons are useful projectiles for atom-smashing experiments,
since they are positively charged particles and therefore can be speeded up
in a cyclotron or a Van de Graaff generator. Alpha particles, or helium nuclei,
are also useful in this connection because diey can be accelerated in the cyclotron.
Alpha particles having high energies may also be obtained from the
natural radioactivity of radium and other elements. Electrons, as speeded
up in the betatron, are useful in producing very penetrating x-rays.

In 1932, another useful atomic projectile was added to the above list--the
nucleus of an isotope of hydrogen of atomic weight 2. This isotope is named
deuterium, or heavy hydrogen. Its nucleus contains one proton and one neutron
and is called a deuteron. With the more recent production of tritium, a
man-made isotope of hydrogen of atomic weight 3, the roll call of hydrogen
atoms now extends to a triplet group rather than twins.

The emergence of the deuteron as an important particle is another example
of skillful detective work by teams of chemists and physicists. Its discovery
was fitting reward for the accurate and painstaking measurements of atomic
weights which had previously been made.
