The total damage resulting from both bombs was considerable. Of the 87
vessels used in the test, eight warships and two landing boats were sunk.
Twenty other ships were seriously damaged. The underwater blast alone
sunk three major ships.

But by far the greatest effect on a real fleet would have been the casualties
inflicted on the crews of vessels by the radioactive effects. Casualties from
this source might have occurred in two ways. In the case of the first explosion
in air, there would have been many deaths from direct exposure to the flash.
Those who escaped this flash because they were protected by some obstruction
might still fall prey to the effects of radioactive products of the explosion.
Ten per cent of the animals used in the air-burst explosion were killed outright.
An additional 5 per cent succumbed to death from radioactivity within
2 weeks.

In the case of the underwater test, the radioactivity effect was more widespread
and it is estimated that crews of vessels up to 4 miles from the
explosion would suffer heavy casualties from this source. The intense radioactive
effect here results from the neutron and gamma-ray emission from the
bomb together with the radioactive products such as salt, water, and iron
produced by the neutron bombardment of these common materials. Radioactive
water is then sprayed high into the air by the explosion to fall as a mist
over surrounding objects or to rush over the ships in waves. The operation
of a boat in such contaminated waters would cause intake of radioactive air
or water, with serious consequences to the crew. Decontamination is extremely
difficult under present procedures, since close approach for any appreciable
time is too dangerous and no ordinary clothing will protect personnel from the
penetrating radiations.

These effects caused Vice-Admiral Blandy, in charge of the tests, to characterize
the bomb as a "form of poison warfare." It now seems that the
possible radioactive effects may become the most terrifying aspect of the atom
bomb.
