
Chapter Nineteen

The Atomic Bomb

During 1939 and early in 1940, scientists of all nations pursued the
quest of attaining large-scale atomic energy in the usual cooperative
manner of science. Any discovery was immediately published in
scientific journals and served to stimulate investigation of new possibilities.
As a result of this widespread activity in many nations, the basic ideas underlying
the chain reaction had become known by 1940. But as the war clouds
darkened, it still remained doubtful whether a chain reaction liberating tremendous
amounts of energy could be achieved with ordinary uranium. During
the war, very little was published in the journals of science concerning this
development. Prior to the war, many conservative scientists considered the
whole subject interesting but impractical and fantastic.

But the report of the atom-bomb test in New Mexico on July 16,1945, coupled
with the dropping of two atomic bombs on Japan, proved that the impractical
and fantastic had become very real.

These explosions revealed to the world an astounding story of scientific
and technical accomplishment that is unequaled in all the history of the
world. Had anyone in 1940 tried to poll technical and scientific opinion on
the possibility of creating atomic bombs within 5 years, the answers would have
revealed considerable skepticism. There were too many obstacles to be overcome.
