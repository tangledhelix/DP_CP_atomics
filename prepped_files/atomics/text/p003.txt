
Chapter One

Enter--the Atomic Age

Man has lived on this earth for a very short time. His development
from the caveman some 20,000 years ago represents only one one-hundred-thousandth
of the 2 billion years that we assign as the
approximate age of the earth. Compressing the time of existence of the earth
into a brief century, man's civilized existence of 20,000 years would be a mere
9 hours. The last 200 years, which have witnessed the intensive development
of science and our rapidly growing industrial civilization, with its autos and
airplanes, telephones and radios, represent about 5 minutes of this condensed
time.

In terms of his own historical development, man is an adolescent. Growing
up with all the bewilderment and lack of confidence of the child new to the
ways of the world, he now approaches a period of great and rapid change.
He looks forward with great hope, yet with deep trepidation. He is unsure of
himself, of his ability to cope with the problems he faces. He lacks the confidence
and the assurance of the adult who knows where he is going.
