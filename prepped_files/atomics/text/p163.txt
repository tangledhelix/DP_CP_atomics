
Chapter Fifteen

Plutonium--Large-scale Alchemy

We have been so engrossed in the remarkable details of the chain-reacting
piles that we never paused to ask, Why build enormous
and vastly expensive piles during the war? Surely we
had sufficient sources of power from waterfalls, coal, and oil. The piles
could not be used as high explosives either. They are very bulky. Furthermore,
the development of energy by the slow neutrons of the uranium-graphite
pile is not rapid enough to make an efficient explosive.

To find the answer, we turn our attention to the uranium 238 atoms in the
pile. Thus far, the U235 atoms have been given the stellar role, since it is the
splitting of these atoms which gives rise to both the chain-carrying neutrons
and the energy release. Now, 99 out of every 100 uranium atoms are U238.
Thus far, we have mentioned that they are neutron robbers and pointed
out that too much of this robbery will stop the chain. But what is the result
of this robbery? We shall soon discover that a new element is formed
plutonium, atomic number 94. And, most important of all, the plutonium behaves
in the same way as U235. It can be split by neutrons and will liberate
large amounts of energy and more neutrons. It appeared very probable
early in the war that it could be used as the ingredient of a very effective
atomic bomb. This chapter tells the story of the discovery and large-scale
preparation of this unusual element.
