
Chapter Twenty-three

Seven-league Boots

Taking our gaze away from the slide rule and the latest Mechanical
Engineer's Handbook, let us dwell briefly on some of the more
imaginative projections of atomic energy that excite our dreams of the
future. If we scoff and jeer, let us remember that the atomic age is only a
few years old. Let us also consider how small our meager successes may appear
when illuminated by the gaze of centuries hence.

We might get a proper perspective by going back a brief hundred years
and trying to picture how the radio, the automobile, the airplane, the rocket,
and the atomic bomb might have been viewed by a reasonable person of that
time. If the incredible has become a reality in this short period, there is no
doubt that many of today's apparently fantastic suggestions for atomic energy
may come about--sooner than might be imagined.

However, we must inject a note of caution to the "buyer" concerning all
schemes using uranium, thorium, and plutonium. In harnessing atomic
energy bulky structures are needed to protect people in the vicinity from the
neutron and gamma radiation. Schemes making use of exploding atomic
bombs carry with them all the radioactivity dangers of Hiroshima, Nagasaki,
and Bikini.
