Why the alchemists failed to make gold . . .

Dalton's atomic theory gave us a partial explanation for the failure of the
alchemist to transmute various elements into gold. According to Dalton the
atoms are fundamental building blocks. They can be built up into molecules
but cannot be broken down into other materials. Nor can these fundamental
blocks be made from other fundamental blocks. Yet that is precisely what
the alchemists of the Middle Ages were attempting to do with gold
the basic elements.

It is still true today that the chemist cannot change one element into another
if he agrees to use the ordinary tools in the laboratory. However, our story
of atomic energy deals mainly with the efforts of those who defied the concept
of Dalton's unchangeable atoms and actually accomplished what he
considered to be impossible. To do so, they had to use rather novel tools such
as radium, atom-smashing machines, and the like. But more of this part of the
story later.
