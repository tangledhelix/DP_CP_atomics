The alpha particles usually end their charged existence by acquiring electrons
from the air around them to become helium atoms. The presence of
helium formed in this way can be detected by that sensitive tool of the
physicist, the spectroscope. In other words, the alpha particle is simply a
helium atom which has lost 2 electrons and is therefore charged positively
(++).

It was also found that these alpha particles are very efficient ionizers. When
traveling through air like a "cannon ball through a wheat field" they rip off
electrons from large numbers of atoms, giving rise to tens of thousands of
electrically charged atoms or ions along their path, thus leaving very heavy
cloud tracks. However, at each collision some speed is lost, until the alpha
particle finally comes to rest after traversing about 1 inch in air. The tracks
left by these alpha particles from radium are all approximately equal in
length, indicating that they have a uniform amount of energy as they come
shooting out.

Other radioactive materials have different ranges, or distances traversed in
air. The alphas from radium C, one of the elements "descended" from radium,
can travel almost 3 inches in air, with each "bullet" creating about 220,000
pairs of ions from air molecules. Most alpha particles, however, are easily
stopped by a sheet of paper, where the molecules are much closer together
than in air.

High-speed electrons, beta particles, also found . . .

A portion of the radiations consists of negatively charged particles shot out
with speeds as high as 160,000 miles per second (almost equal to the velocity
of light). These are called beta particles. Experiments using Thomson's
methods of applying electric and magnetic fields in deflection studies soon
showed that these particles had a negative charge and a mass approximately
1/1,850 that of the hydrogen atom. In other words, beta particles are electrons.
Because they have a smaller mass, they are more readily deflected than alpha
particles, and as a result their paths are more zigzag in nature. Since they
suffer fewer collisions per inch, they can travel a much greater distance before
being stopped.
